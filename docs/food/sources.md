# Food list sources

Every number in `mobile/src/data/food/foods.ts` names its source in the food's `sources` list. The keys below are the `source` field of those entries ([ADR 0353](../adr/0353-ugandan-food-list.md)).

## Composition (kcal, available carbohydrate, protein, fat, fibre per 100 g)

| Key              | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Used for                                                                                                                                                                  | How it is cited                                                                                        |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `KE-FCT-2018`    | FAO/Government of Kenya. 2018. _Kenya Food Composition Tables_. Nairobi, 254 pp. (the PDF's recommended citation). PDF: https://www.fao.org/3/i8897en/I8897EN.pdf (record: https://openknowledge.fao.org/handle/20.500.14283/i8897en)                         | 165 foods: staples, mixed dishes (ugali, matoke, stews, chapati, mandazi), vegetables, fruits, meat, fish, milk, fats                                                     | Food code and name, e.g. `04074 Kale (sukuma wiki), boiled, drained (without salt)`, with the PDF page |
| `WAFCT-2019`     | Vincent A., Grande F., Compaoré E. et al. 2020. _FAO/INFOODS Food Composition Table for Western Africa (2019)_. FAO, Rome. Datasheets: https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/faoweb/2020/WAFCT_2019.xlsx                                                  | 2 foods: millet beer, maize beer                                                                                                                                          | Food code and name, sheet `03 NV_sum_39 (per 100g EP)`                                                 |
| `USDA-SR-LEGACY` | U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service. _FoodData Central: SR Legacy_, April 2018 release (public domain). https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/ — download: https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/fdc-datasets/FoodData_Central_sr_legacy_food_csv_2018-04.zip | 13 foods with no East or West African row (taro, gonja, crisps, popcorn, roasted groundnuts, tea, coffee, sodas, juices, lager, spirits) and 60 household portion weights | FDC id, NDB number and description; portions by `food_portion` id                                      |

The two African tables report **available** carbohydrate directly. USDA reports carbohydrate by difference, which includes fibre, so for USDA foods `carbsG` = carbohydrate by difference − total dietary fibre (both as published). Values in square brackets in the source tables (less precise values) are used as given; "tr" (trace) is stored as 0.

## Glycemic index

| Key          | Source                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 | Used for                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `GI-2008`    | Atkinson F.S., Foster-Powell K., Brand-Miller J.C. 2008. International tables of glycemic index and glycemic load values: 2008. _Diabetes Care_ 31(12): 2281–2283. doi:10.2337/dc08-1239. Open: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2584181/ — online appendix (Tables A1, A2) via https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC2584181/supplementaryFiles | 34 foods from **Table 1** (average GI of 62 common foods across studies) and 11 from **Table A1** (subjects with normal glucose tolerance), cited by item number and appendix page                                                                                                                       |
| `KE-GI-2021` | Ebere R., Imungi J., Kimani V. 2021. Glycemic index values of traditional Kenyan foods: the missing link in the effectiveness of dietary approach in the prevention and management of diabetes mellitus in Kenya. _African Health Sciences_ 21(2): 710–718. doi:10.4314/ahs.v21i2.29. Open: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8568238/                          | 6 foods from its **Table 1** (healthy subjects, glucose = 100): whole-maize ugali, boiled white rice, rice with beans, bean stew, sweet potato, boiled cassava. The original measurements are Ebere R.A., Imungi J.K., Kimani V.N. 2017, _Food Science and Quality Management_ 63: 7–12, 55–63 and 64–73 |

Order of preference for a GI: an East African measurement of the same food, then the 2008 Table 1 average, then a single Table A1 entry (African studies first). Only values on the glucose scale from subjects without diabetes are used.

## Reached but not used

- **International tables of GI and GL values 2021** (Atkinson et al., _Am J Clin Nutr_ 2021; 114: 1625–1632, doi:10.1093/ajcn/nqab233). The paper is not open access and its supplementary tables could not be fetched from the publisher; copies on third-party sites were not used. The 2008 tables stand in.
- **Amwoma et al. 2023**, GI of maize, millet and sorghum ugali (_Advances in Public Health_, doi:10.1155/2023/6641966). The publisher page sits behind a bot check, so its numbers could not be read; millet and sorghum bread keep GI unknown.
- **Table 2 of Ebere et al. 2021** (maize and millet porridge, white rice, cassava): measured in people with type 2 diabetes, so not used.
- **The Food Carb Counting Guide, Uganda** (Sseguya W., 2014, https://www.eresourcesforhealth.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/The-Uganda-Food-Carb-Counting-Guide.pdf). It gives portions by description and GI as arrows but no gram weights or GI numbers, so nothing was taken from it.

## Rebuilding the list

`docs/food/build/` holds the scripts that made `foods.ts`. `spec.py` names the source row for each food; `gen.py` reads the numbers from the parsed tables, checks every Table A1 GI against the appendix line and every USDA portion weight against `food_portion`, and prints the TypeScript. The downloads are not committed; fetch them from the links above, then run `pdftxt.py` (Kenya PDF and GI appendix), `kenya_parse.py`, `wafct_dump.py`, `usda_dump.py`, `html2txt.py` (the two PMC articles) and `gen.py`, and format with the repo's prettier.
