# Lab Phase 5 research notes: patient access to results, delta checks and autoverification, the infection prevention feed

Compiled 17 Oct 2026 for `PROMPT-LAB-PHASE-5.md`. Three parts, each with its own sources and confidence marks (H = primary source read directly; M = secondary or partial; L = inferred or weak). Several primary texts could not be read in full (DPPA Regulations 2021, the National HTS Policy 2022, CLSI EP33 and AUTO15 body text, ISO 15189:2022 7.4.1.5 wording, IDSR 3rd ed. Tables 1–3, Uganda IPC Guidelines 2025 Chapters 5–6); each part lists them under "Not verified".

- **Part A** — Patients seeing their results: DPPA 2019, the Patients' Charter, the HIV Act, minors, SMS rules (UCC S.I. 83 of 2019), LabRDS, US and UK practice, display evidence.
- **Part B** — Delta checks and autoverification: CLSI EP33 2nd ed., AUTO15, ISO 15189:2022, CAP, SLIPTA v3, RCV and the EFLM database, published performance, wrong blood in tube.
- **Part C** — The infection prevention feed: Uganda IPC Guidelines 2025, WHO core components, IDSR 3rd ed., NAP-AMR II, GLASS and WHONET, alert organism lists, precautions by result, evidence for electronic alerts.

**Headlines for the build:** no Ugandan law requires immediate release, so clinician review before release is lawful, but holds must not block a DPPA s.24 access request. HIV results go only through a practitioner with post-test counselling. No sourced default delta limits exist; ship a calculator, not numbers. The IPC recipient in Uganda's 2025 guidelines is the IPC Link Nurse; IDSR notifications go to the surveillance focal person.

## What this means for MedSync (analysis, not from sources)

1. **The legal basis to build exists, but nothing requires immediate release.** Under DPPA s.24 and Patients' Charter Art. 16, patients are entitled to their own medical information, including a copy, within 30 days. Ugandan law has nothing like the US Cures Act. So MedSync can keep its "released by a clinician or lab" gate and is not legally obliged to auto-release. To stay within the 30-day limit and the HIDHSP Strategy 1.6 aim, results should not sit unreleased indefinitely. Suggest an aged-hold alert, for example at 7 days.
2. **Model withholding as therapeutic privilege, not a blanket rule.** Charter Art. 10 (likely "severe harm") and MHA s.57(2) (serious prejudice) allow clinician-level withholding. The DPPA has no harm exception, so a withhold is a per-result clinician decision, recorded with a reason and auditable. It should be reviewable on request, in the spirit of 45 CFR 171.201(e). Model it as a `hold` with `reason`, `held_by`, and `review_requested`.
3. **Never auto-release HIV diagnostic results to the portal.** HIV Act s.3(1) requires post-test counselling "in all cases", and s.18(1) releases results only through a practitioner to the person tested. Penalties under s.42 are criminal. The release gate for the HIV category should require a "post-test counselling done" record. Treat VL and other HIV-linked tests (CD4, EID PCR) as a sensitive category: clinician release only, opt-in to portal view, never in SMS. LabRDS shows MoH accepts electronic VL return to clients behind PIN and ART number, but whether clients see values is unconfirmed.
4. **Hold sensitive categories by default, mirroring NHS practice.** These include HIV, hepatitis B and C serology if the programme policy says so, syphilis and STIs, pregnancy (hCG), TB (GeneXpert), and anything ordered by mental health. They need clinician release and are excluded from guardian proxy for ages 12–17. No Ugandan rule names these categories. This is a product safety choice, so it should go to the hospital's clinical governance for sign-off.
5. **Keep the SMS content-free and use LabRDS wording.** For example: "<Facility>: your test results are ready. Log in to MedSync or visit the facility." No test name, no value, no clinic name that reveals a condition: no "ART clinic" or "TB clinic" as sender ID or in the text. Use one neutral facility-level sender ID, budgeted at UGX 250,000 a month through Africa's Talking, registered with MTN and Airtel. Include a STOP route (reg 12), even though transactional health SMS is arguably not "commercial". Send between 06:00 and 19:00 except for critical alerts. Government (MoH) facilities are exempt under reg 2(2); private facilities are not. Store explicit SMS consent per patient (DPPA s.9: consent for health data).
6. **Proxy and age rules.**
   - Under 12: guardian proxy gets full access, matching HIV Act s.18(2)(a).
   - Ages 12–17: the adolescent may have their own account. Guardian proxy is limited to non-sensitive categories, and adolescent HIV, SRH and pregnancy results never go to the guardian by default.
   - 18 and over: no proxy without the patient's written consent (s.18(2)(c) analogue).
   - Under 18, account creation needs parental or guardian consent, except where the law allows otherwise (DPPA s.8). This 12–17 split conflicts in part with DPPA s.8. Get a Ugandan legal opinion before going live.
7. **Results display.**
   - Show a number line with the reference range rather than a table (Zikmund-Fisher 2017).
   - Add a harm-anchor band ("most doctors are not concerned until here") only where clinicians have agreed the threshold for that analyte (2018).
   - Pair the display with a one-line plain-language meaning, an action line ("No action needed", "Your clinician will contact you", "Come to the facility today"), and an optional clinician comment. Design alone does not prevent misreading (Fraccaro 2018).
   - Critical values should never be patient-first. Phone contact by staff comes first, then release.
8. **Worry is real but small.** Steitz found 16.5% more worried with abnormal results, yet 95% preferred immediate release. So an optional patient setting, "notify me only after my clinician has reviewed", is a reasonable later addition. It is not needed for Phase 5 because results are already clinician-released.
9. **Audit and breach handling.** Log every patient view of a result (HIV Act s.20 requires telling the tested person about disclosures). Wire a breach path to the PDPO "immediately" (DPPA s.23; Regulation 33).

## What this means for MedSync (analysis, not from sources)

1. **The delta-check config model should follow EP33's three parameters**: per test (and specimen matrix), set the _type_ (absolute / percent / rate-absolute / rate-percent / RCV), the _limit_ (with optional asymmetric up/down limits) and the _look-back window_. Store the _basis_ of each limit (BV/RCV with CVA and CVI and their sources, patient-data percentile, or clinical) and who approved it. The evidence does not support one optimal window (Tan 2020), so the window should be configurable per test with no hidden default.
2. **Ship no numeric defaults.** The only "example limits" found are unsourced. Offer an RCV calculator: the lab enters its own IQC CV (MedSync already holds this from QC), picks a CVI with a citation to the EFLM BV database, and MedSync computes symmetric or log-normal RCV at a chosen Z. Also offer a patient-data percentile option once enough local history exists. Both need lab-director approval before activation.
3. **Delta checks are decision support, not blocks.** Most alerts are physiological, treatment- or transfusion-related (Q-Probes: about 60%; AKU: 95% invalid). Show the previous value, the time gap, the computed delta and any likely explanation the system knows about (e.g. a transfusion recorded in the interval, dialysis, a drug). Record a structured outcome for every alert (accepted as physiological, recollect, previous specimen suspect, contamination, mislabel). That gives the EP33 Ch 7 performance review and the PPV for each rule.
4. **The investigation workflow must prompt checking the previous specimen.** Q-Probes found previous specimens implicated in 23.8% of problems yet rarely checked.
5. **Build in pattern rules beside univariate deltas**, e.g. a K↑ + Ca↓ (± Mg↓/ALP↓) EDTA-contamination flag and a dilution/drip-arm pattern (many analytes falling together, glucose rising). Make the patterns configurable and describe them qualitatively, without inventing thresholds. Cornes (25% of K ≥ 6.0 samples contaminated) justifies holding hyperkalaemia for review.
6. **Panel-level delta logic is needed for autoverification.** Per-analyte deltas across a panel multiply false positives (Tan, Ann Clin Biochem 2020). Support a "hold if ≥ k of N analytes fail" rule as an option.
7. **The selective autoverification gate should, at minimum, mirror the union of the sources**: QC acceptable _and current_ for that analyte on that instrument (CAP GEN.43878, Wang 2019); no analyser error flags (AUTO15 §2.3); within AMR; within reference interval / AV limits; not critical; delta passed or no prior result (CAP GEN.43890 requires the same deltas as manual release); HIL below threshold; specimen age valid; not modified after first save; no open non-conformity; no comment. OpenELIS treats missing QC as "neither clears nor blocks". **MedSync should be stricter and hold when no current QC exists**, given the existing Westgard release-hold design.
8. **Compliance features to build**: versioned rule sets with director approval and effective dates; a validation harness that replays historical results and compares AV decisions with manual decisions (sensitivity/specificity, as in Wang 2019, Zhu 2022 and Jin 2021), with a "shadow mode" before go-live; annual revalidation reminders plus revalidation on any analyser, interface or rule change (CAP GEN.43875, ISO 7.4.1.5); a **one-click rapid suspension** per test, instrument or whole lab, with a reason and audit; identification of autoverified results in the audit trail and the result record (GEN.43887, ISO 7.4.1.5) with date and time; and an end-to-end interface test whenever an analyser message set changes (the CAP Today haemolysis-message failure).
9. **Uganda context**: A-LIS (v3, 2021) has none of these features, and OpenELIS has partial auto-release without delta checks. Configurable delta checks plus guarded AV would differentiate MedSync locally. Evidence from sub-Saharan Africa is absent, so MedSync should log outcomes from day one; that data could support a local publication. Transfusion is the main source of false-positive Hb/MCV deltas in high-transfusion settings (AKU), so linking to blood-bank issue records is worth doing.
10. **Exclusions**: AUTO15 excludes transfusion medicine and microbiology from autoverification. MedSync should block AV for blood bank (group and screen, crossmatch) and microbiology by design. For blood group, the "historical group vs current group" comparison is the transfusion analogue of a delta check, and it supports the two-sample rule.

## What this means for MedSync (analysis, not from sources)

1. **Two routes, two recipients.** (a) An **IDSR notifiable route** goes to the _facility surveillance focal person_: immediate for epidemic-prone or immediately reportable results (VHF, cholera, measles, anthrax, plague, yellow fever, RVF, meningococcal disease, etc.), with a pre-filled IDSR case-based form (Annex 2F) and lab form (Annex 2G), and weekly roll-up rows for the Monday 12:00 DHIS2 deadline. Lab-confirmed MDR/XDR-TB is itself a weekly-reportable item. (b) An **IPC alert route** goes to the _IPC Link Nurse_ and IPC committee. That is the role name the 2025 Uganda guidelines use, and its duties already include HAI surveillance "in collaboration with laboratory services". Some results trigger both routes (e.g. RR-TB, cholera, VHF).
2. **Alerts that work without microbiology:** Xpert MTB+ (airborne precautions; RIF-resistant also goes to the MDR-TB weekly report), measles IgM/PCR+ (airborne), cholera RDT/culture+ (contact if incontinent, plus IDSR), _C. difficile_ toxin+ (contact), VHF suspicion or PCR+ (isolation + IDSR immediate), meningococcal (droplet), and SARS-CoV-2/influenza+ (droplet/airborne by policy). Each alert should carry the **suggested precaution type plus the stop criterion** from CDC Appendix A, e.g. TB: 3 negative smears and clinical improvement.
3. **The alert-organism catalogue should be configurable and versioned, seeded in tiers.** Tier 1, alert in every setting: CPE/CRE, CRAB, CP-_Pseudomonas_, MRSA, VRE, _C. auris_, MDR/RR-TB, _C. difficile_, invasive GAS, VISA/VRSA and linezolid-R (CARAlert-style "critical, confirm at NMRL"). Tier 2, high-risk units only (NICU, ICU, oncology, burns): ESBL-E, environmental gram-negatives. This matches NIPCM's split and the WHO BPPL 2024 critical/high groups. Phenotype flags (ESBL, carbapenemase, MRSA screen) map straight to WHONET columns.
4. **Readmission flag.** The strongest evidence (Kac, Quan, Kho, VA) is for a _persistent patient-level MDRO flag_ that fires on readmission or transfer, ideally auto-ordering precautions rather than just emailing. Build the flag as part of the patient record, with an IPC-controlled clear date (e.g. after ≥3 negative screens).
5. **Confirmation loop.** Unusual resistance (carbapenemase, vancomycin/linezolid non-susceptibility, _C. auris_) should be marked "presumptive", with an **isolate-referral task to the NMRL** (sentinel-site indicator: 100% of isolates sent; feedback within 4 weeks) and a status field for the NMRL result.
6. **WHONET/GLASS export.** Export isolate-level CSV in WHONET column order, with stable lab, specimen and isolate IDs, a **date of admission** (to derive hospital vs community origin), ward/location type, specimen type coded to GLASS categories (blood, CSF, urine, stool, LRT, genital swabs), phenotype flags, and per-drug columns with guideline and method in the code. Apply GLASS deduplication (first isolate per patient × specimen type × pathogen × period) in the **aggregate** view only, never in the raw export.
7. **HAI surveillance support.** The WHO minimum requirements mean only tertiary facilities need active HAI surveillance with AMR; secondary facilities follow the national plan. MedSync should give HAI denominators (patient-days, device-days, operations for SSI) and positive cultures linked to device/procedure timing, and let the IPC team adjudicate HAI against the Uganda definitions once the national plan is public. The Entebbe/Hoima/Kayunga SSI pilot (WHO criteria, follow-up on days 6/14/30) is the nearest working model.
8. **Weekly IPC digest** to the Link Nurse and committee: new alerts, open flags, isolates awaiting NMRL confirmation, and HAI candidates. This meets CC4's "timely feedback to stakeholders, especially administration".
