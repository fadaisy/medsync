## 1. Three decisions for ratification

### (a) HbA1c policy — when it may diagnose

**Recommendation: permit HbA1c to diagnose only when the site declares an NGSP-certified, DCCT-standardised assay AND no interfering condition is recorded. Otherwise require plasma glucose. Never infer "no interference" from an unasked question.**

The research changed my view on one point, and it is the important one:

> **NGSP:** interference from HbS, HbC, HbE and HbD traits, raised HbF and carbamylated Hb is **assay-specific, not universal** — the answer depends on which analyser the laboratory runs.

So "HbA1c is unreliable in sickle-cell trait" is _not_ a blanket clinical fact; it is a property of the machine. That has a direct design consequence: **assay identity belongs in the site profile**, and where it is unknown the tool must fail safe toward plasma glucose rather than assume a non-interfering analyser.

Two further findings mean the flag should state a _direction_, not just "unreliable":

- anything that **shortens red-cell survival** — haemolysis, malaria, transfusion — falsely **lowers** HbA1c, so it can mask diabetes;
- **iron-deficiency anaemia** falsely **raises** it, so it can manufacture one.

A single "unreliable" badge would leave a clinician unable to tell which error they are looking at.

ADA 2026 Rec. 2.4 requires plasma-glucose criteria wherever the A1C–glycaemia relationship is altered, and explicitly names HIV/antiretroviral therapy, anaemia, erythropoietin, haemodialysis and pregnancy from 15 weeks. WHO 2011 names haemoglobinopathies, certain anaemias and malaria, and adds a point worth heeding for a resource-limited tier: it judges HbA1c **unaffordable in most low- and middle-income settings**.

### (b) Standard precedence when ADA and WHO disagree

**Recommendation: precedence is per-site data (`SiteProfile.standardPreference`), defaulting to WHO/IDF on a resource-limited profile and ADA on a full profile — and the engine states which standard produced every verdict, on the face of the output.**

This is not a diplomatic dodge; the disagreements are substantive and a silent default would be wrong somewhere:

- **Impaired fasting glucose.** ADA 5.6–6.9 mmol/L; WHO and IDF 6.1–6.9. WHO reviewed the 5.6 cut-point in 2005 and explicitly declined it. Between 5.6 and 6.1 a patient is prediabetic under one standard and normal under the other.
- **Whether IFG requires a normal 2-hour glucose.** WHO/IDF impose it; ADA does not.
- **Whether the two confirmatory results may come from one draw.** ADA allows it explicitly; WHO asks for a repeat on a subsequent day, preferably the same method. **I recommend defaulting to the stricter WHO position for asymptomatic patients** — a same-draw diagnosis is the more consequential error.

And one that should not be configurable at all:

- **The HbA1c prediabetes band 5.7–6.4% is ADA's alone.** WHO holds there is _insufficient evidence to interpret HbA1c below 6.5%_, partly on assay-quality grounds in exactly the settings we are targeting. If we surface that band it must be labelled **ADA** on screen and never presented as consensus.

### (c) The management boundary

**Recommendation: guideline-linked steps with citations only. Anything with a dose sets `requiresPrescribing` and hands off to the prescribing module. No dose is encoded in the catalog or computed by the engine.**

The research supports this cleanly — it captured management as _steps and their triggering conditions_, not doses, and it surfaced that ADA now directs agent choice in type 2 diabetes by comorbidity (ASCVD, heart failure, CKD) **independently of A1C**, displacing a strict metformin-first ladder. WHO PEN gives glucose surrogate targets for settings where HbA1c is unavailable — which is the resource-limited adaptation we need, and it slots into the same step model.

## 2. What the audit did not confirm — all four

None is a wrong number. All four are attribution errors, in a body of work whose entire value is traceable attribution.

1. **WHO 2011 confirmatory rule — invented section name.** The quoted text is real and verbatim, but the cited section _"Recommendations for diagnostic practice"_ does not exist in the document. Correct locator: Chapter 2, closing paragraphs.
2. **WHO 2011 HbA1c cut-point — fabricated unit.** Recorded as "6.5% (48 mmol/mol)". The audit grepped the retrieved PDF for "mmol/mol" and "IFCC" and found **zero hits**. WHO 2011 gives the percentage only; the 48 mmol/mol equivalence must be sourced to WHO 2019 or ADA 2026.
3. **DKA venous-vs-arterial sampling — misattributed.** ADA SOC 2026 Table 16.1 gives pH < 7.3 and/or bicarbonate < 18 mmol/L with **no** sampling-site designation. The venous-sampling recommendation belongs to the 2024 consensus report, not the ADA table.
4. **IWGDF foot risk — false independent corroboration.** The claim was that ADA SOC Section 12 _independently adopts_ the IWGDF stratification. ADA Table 12.1 is an **adapted reprint published with permission** from the IWGDF guidelines. It provides no independent confirmation.

**Item 4 is the one that mattered most.** It would have presented a single source appearing twice as two agreeing sources — manufactured confidence, invisible on inspection.

## 3. Conflicts worth your attention

Thirty-five in total; these are the ones that change what we build.

**Highest coding risk — HHS osmolality.** The number **320 attaches to different quantities across standards**: ADA/EASD 2024 uses _effective_ osmolality > 300 mOsm/kg _or_ _total_ > 320; JBDS uses total ≥ 320 only; superseded ADA 2009 used _effective_ > 320 with glucose in mg/dL divided by 18, where the current formula takes mmol/L. Any stored result must record which formula produced it.

**DKA thresholds differ between ADA/EASD and JBDS.** Bicarbonate < 18 vs < 15 mmol/L; HHS glucose ≥ 33.3 vs ≥ 30.0 mmol/L; severe-DKA bicarbonate < 10 vs < 5.0 — a two-fold gap. A patient with bicarbonate 16, BOHB 3.5 and pH 7.32 **is** DKA under ADA/EASD and **is not** under JBDS.

**Do not build "type 5 / malnutrition-related diabetes" as a selectable diagnosis.** IDF endorsed it in 2025; WHO dropped MRDM in 1999 for lack of evidence and has not reinstated it; ADA has no such category. IDF's own page concedes **no standard diagnostic criteria exist yet**. An unvalidated category here would drive real management decisions.

**Do build ketosis-prone type 2 as its own category** (WHO 2019 hybrid form). The management implication is specific and safety-relevant: insulin at presentation, planned reassessment for withdrawal, counselling on recurrence.

**LADA is structurally different between standards** — a distinct WHO hybrid category, subsumed under type 1 by ADA. Store WHO as the primary axis with an ADA mapping field.

**Retinopathy screening interval is genuinely unsettled**: AAO says yearly, ICO says 1–2 years in both resource tiers, WHO PEN says every 2 years.

## 4. Unsafe to automate — my recommendation

- **Any dose.** Handled by (c) above.
- **Type 5 / malnutrition-related diabetes.** No diagnostic criteria exist to encode.
- **A cardiovascular risk score presented without its validated population.** The schema already warns on this; several widely-used models are not validated in sub-Saharan African populations, and a borrowed number reads as a local one.
- **Auto-classifying T1 vs T2 from phenotype** where C-peptide and antibodies are unavailable. Return `unclassified`.
- **Anything from the 76 `needsCsoInput` findings** until you have ruled on them individually.
