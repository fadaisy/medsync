# Diabetes examination layer — handover for clinical review

> **Nothing here has been ratified.** Every citation in the catalog is `csoSigned: false`, the
> surface is gated behind `NCD_CLERKSHIP_LIVE`, and the profile shows a gated-dark notice while
> any citation is unsigned. This document is what a Clinical Safety Officer reads _before_
> deciding whether any of it may be shown to a clinician.

Built under [ADR 0083](../../adr/0083-examination-discrimination-layer.md), which extends the NCD
protocol engine of [ADR 0082](../../adr/0082-ncd-protocol-engine.md).

## What this is, and what it is not

The examination layer records the physical findings of a diabetes examination and orders a
**bounded** set of conditions by how well the recorded findings support each one. It is decision
support. It does not diagnose, it carries no dose, and every panel it renders says so.

Three properties are structural rather than a matter of care:

1. **The condition list is closed.** Ranking can only ever name a code in
   `lib/ncd/catalog/diabetes/conditions.ts`. Widening the differential means adding an entry
   there, deliberately, with a source. Mimics that lie outside diabetes — salicylate toxicity,
   sepsis with a lactic acidosis — are carried as **named strings**, never as rankable codes, so
   the engine never implies it reasons about something it has no criteria for.
2. **The output is an order, not a probability.** The integers the ranker sorts by are a fixed,
   disease-generic ladder in engine code. They are never stored and never rendered.
3. **Every rank carries its evidence.** A ranked condition cannot be constructed without the
   finding codes that raised it _and_ the codes that argued against it.

## How to review it

Read these two generated files rather than the code:

- [`diabetes-exam-index.md`](diabetes-exam-index.md) — every id, by section, with what each
  finding discriminates.
- [`diabetes-exam-sources.md`](diabetes-exam-sources.md) — every distinct source, what it is cited
  for, and every `needsCsoInput` declaration.

Both are regenerated by `npm run ncd:manifest` and must never be hand-edited. `npm run ncd:check`
prints validation issues and exits non-zero on any error.

The golden vignettes in `tests/ncd/` are the other review artefact: a synthetic patient in, an
expected profile out. **If a vignette's expectation is clinically wrong, the catalog is wrong** —
and the argument can be had in clinical language rather than in TypeScript.

## Open questions — the CSO decides these

1. **Age-band boundaries are convention, not catalog data.** `lib/ncd/age.ts` bands a date of
   birth at 28 days, 1, 10, 20 and 65 years. Ten and twenty are WHO's definition of adolescence;
   65 is where ADA's older-adults section applies. They live in engine code rather than posing as
   a cited `Citation`, and they are pinned by test so changing them is deliberate. Ratify or replace.
2. **ISPAD Chapter 11 is cited at two different levels of verification.** `conditions.ts` quotes
   it for the paediatric DKA severity cut-points and the cerebral-injury incidence, reporting a
   full retrieval. `exam-cns.ts` could not reproduce that retrieval and uses BSPED instead. The
   chapter could not be fetched independently while writing this note. Obtain it and reconcile.
3. **Waist-circumference scale.** The catalog carries a four-band table (80/100/120 cm men,
   70/90/110 cm women) whose primary source was never retrieved, and it **replaced** the published
   WHO expert-consultation scale (94/102, 80/88), which gives different answers for the same
   patient. Decide which scale this site uses. Ethnicity is still not recorded at all, and IDF
   publishes lower thresholds for several populations.
4. **Hypoglycaemia threshold — three bodies, three numbers.** ADA Table 6.4 level 1 < 3.9 mmol/L
   and level 2 < 3.0; UCG 1.1.6 says symptoms generally occur below 3.0; WHO PEN-H acts at
   ≤ 2.8. The catalog must apply one per site profile.
5. **A label that overstates its own rule.** The `dka_bohb` criterion in `complications.ts` is
   _labelled_ "…in known diabetes or glucose ≥ 11.1 mmol/L" but no predicate tests glucose. This
   is what makes euglycaemic DKA establishable by the same criterion, so it may well be
   deliberate — but the label promises a test the rule does not perform. Confirm the intent.
6. **Standard bodies with no slot in the enum.** BSPED, JBDS, ISPAD, IDSA, ECMM, StatPearls and
   the Ugandan MoH all ship as `standard: "local"` with the gap declared. If any of them is to
   carry authority at a site, the enum needs to name it.
7. **Whether paediatric diabetes is in scope at all.** Neonatal, infant and child capture is
   deferred. The type declares those bands so the content has a home, and nothing currently
   populates them.

## ADA / Uganda Clinical Guidelines divergences recorded

Recorded rather than smoothed over, per the brief. Where the two texts disagree, **both are cited
on the entry** and the entry says which it follows.

- ADA rec 12.18 does not mention the ankle jerk; WHO HEARTS-D makes 'unequivocally decreased or absent ankle reflexes' one of three findings whose combination (two or more) defines probable peripheral neuropathy. `ankle_reflexes` exists because of that divergence — it is the only component test needing no instrument.
- ADA specifies instrument, modality and interval (10 g monofilament annually plus temperature-or-pinprick and 128 Hz vibration). Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 specifies none of the three: its whole chronic-complication instruction is 'Assess for complications (renal disease, eye problems, diabetic foot, peripheral neuropathy, heart problem, stroke), and refer/ treat accordingly' at health-centre level III. Recorded as the feature-level citation on `ankle_reflexes` (WHO carries the discrimination), with a needsCsoInput saying it is cited for a divergence and not for a threshold.
- IWGDF uses the monofilament to predict ULCERATION; the only published LRs available use nerve conduction studies as reference standard and therefore describe DETECTION of neuropathy. The LRs are attached to the neuropathy claim only and the citation says so.
- GENITAL CANDIDIASIS — UCG 2023 §8.1.3 lists 'itchy genitalia (candidiasis)' among the symptoms of diabetes, which ADA Section 2's classic-symptom list does not. But UCG §3.2.8.1, the entry a clinician actually reaches for balanitis, sits in the STI chapter, names only Candida and Trichomonas, treats and advises hygiene/circumcision, and directs NO blood-glucose test. Both halves are cited on the finding: the Ugandan diabetes chapter is the feature's own `citation` (it is why the field exists here) and StatPearls carries the evidential claim and the instruction to test the glucose after recurrent episodes. Recorded in the file header and in UCG_2023_BALANITIS.needsCsoInput, which states plainly that it is cited for an absence.
- INJECTION SITES — ADA Section 9 requires the administration sites to be examined for lipohypertrophy and the patient taught rotation. UCG 2023 gives four pages of insulin regimens and, in the full retrieved text of all 1,161 pages, contains no injection-site examination, no rotation instruction and no mention of lipohypertrophy (the string 'rotat' does not appear). Recorded in ADA_S9_ROTATION.needsCsoInput so the CSO sees that both injection-site fields are ADA content in a Ugandan catalog before signing them.
- Hypoglycaemia threshold, three bodies three numbers: ADA Table 6.4 level 1 <3.9 mmol/L and level 2 <3.0; UCG 1.1.6 "symptoms generally occur with a blood glucose <3.0 mmol/L (55 mg/dl)"; WHO PEN-H "If blood glucose is < = 2.8 mmol/l treat hypoglycemia". All three recorded in the file header; NONE asserted on a feature, because a threshold is a rule (ADR 0083).
- The autonomic/neuroglycopenic split: ADA and JBDS separate the two and JBDS states the mechanism; UCG lists them as one undifferentiated set of "clinical features" with confusion among the EARLY symptoms beside tremor and sweating — so a UCG-trained clinician has no vocabulary for the distinction impaired awareness turns on. That is the stated reason the two halves are two findings, not one checklist.
- The hyperosmolar brain: UCG 8.1.4 lists "Alterated consciousness, coma" under DKA and gives HHS only slower onset, worse dehydration and no ketosis — no cognitive change at all; ADA Table 16.2 records "Change in cognitive state common" and JBDS ties it to osmolality >330 mOsm/kg. Both readings carried on `disorientation` (discriminates + importantNegative caveat).
- The stroke that is a low: WHO PEN-H puts a blood glucose inside the EXAMINATION of a one-sided deficit to exclude hypoglycaemia or DKA; UCG 4.1.11 states no differential for stroke and directs "Control blood sugar with insulin if diabetic". The catalog follows PEN-H and says so.
- ADA vs UCG (1): UCG's diabetes chapter has no examination step at all — no orthostatic BP, no capillary refill, no weight against a previous one. ADA Table 4.1 names orthostatic measures 'when indicated'. The fields are ADA's and are offered, not demanded.
- ADA/BSPED vs UCG (2): UCG's DKA/HHS section gives 'Cardiovascular collapse (hypotension)' as the circulatory feature and names no perfusion measure, where BSPED calls hypotension 'a late sign of shock' and puts the central capillary refill ahead of it. UCG does carry a >2-second slow-refill threshold — but in its severe-malaria chapter (§2.5.3.3, p 176), not the diabetes one. Both recorded, on UCG_2023_CRT and BSPED_2021_SHOCK.
- ADA vs UCG (3): on beta-blockade — the commonest false positive for a blunted postural pulse rise — UCG §8.1.3 (p 474) says outright 'Do not use beta blockers, e.g., atenolol in diabetes' where ADA carries no blanket caution. Recorded in the file header.
- ADA vs UCG (4): on weight loss, ADA reads unintentional loss as a discriminator WITHIN diabetes pointing at type 1; UCG lists it as an ordinary feature and puts HIV/AIDS and TB in the differential of the same presentation. Both cited on documented_weight_loss (ADA_S2_T1_PHENOTYPE / UCG_2023_DM_FEATURES), and the tier is `moderate` rather than `strong` because of it. HIV and TB are deliberately given no rankable code.
- ADA vs the 2011 consensus on the definition itself: ADA writes strict inequalities (>20/>10) and folds the heart-rate response into the definition; the consensus statement usually cited writes 'at least' 20/10 and speaks of pressure alone. Only ADA's was retrieved, so only ADA's is quoted.
- THE DIVERGENCE HERE IS NOT ADA-vs-UCG BUT BOTH-vs-THE-LITERATURE, and it is recorded in the file header and in UCG_2023_COMPLICATIONS.needsCsoInput. ADA SOC 2026 Section 4 was retrieved in full: its comorbidity chapter covers bone health at length (fracture risk rec 4.8, individualised targets rec 4.11, calcium/vitamin D rec 4.12) and contains no cheiroarthropathy, Dupuytren, frozen shoulder, trigger finger or carpal tunnel; Section 12 names no entrapment neuropathy either.
- Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 was downloaded in full and all 1,161 pages searched: 'cheiro', 'prayer sign', 'Dupuytren', 'frozen shoulder', 'adhesive capsulit', 'trigger finger', 'carpal tunnel', 'joint mobility' and 'tenosynovitis' return ZERO occurrences anywhere in the document, and its own chronic-complication list (quoted verbatim in the file, p 472) is purely micro- and macrovascular. So no guideline in the catalog's precedence order backs this segment at all — every discrimination rests on primary literature and every elicitation on a tertiary reference work, which is why nothing here is graded above `moderate` and most is `weak`.
- ADA Section 12 supplies the one governing-guideline sentence the file uses ('Diabetic neuropathy is a diagnosis of exclusion. Nondiabetic neuropathies may be present in people with diabetes and may be treatable') — it names the principle, not carpal tunnel syndrome; the file says so rather than implying ADA names the entrapment.
- REFERRAL TIMING: ADA 12.9 says refer 'promptly' for any macular oedema, moderate-or-worse NPDR or any PDR, and attaches no interval. ICO 2017 Table 2a/2b attaches numbers and they differ by resource setting — moderate NPDR re-examined 3–6 months (high resource) vs 6–12 months (low/intermediate), referral required in both; severe NPDR <3 months; PDR <1 month; noncentral DME 3 months; central-involved DME 1 month. Both recorded in the file header; ADA's instruction is the danger-sign action because it fires on a grade alone, and ICO's intervals are NOT encoded as screening intervals (that belongs to a Complication, which this file does not own).
- SCREENING FREQUENCY: ADA 12.3/12.4/12.5 — dilated comprehensive exam 5 years after onset in type 1, at diagnosis in type 2, annually thereafter, 1–2 yearly only if no retinopathy and glycaemia at goal. WHO PEN-H — 'Eyes should be screened for eye disease (diabetic retinopathy) at the time of diagnosis and at least every two years thereafter', for everyone, with no glycaemic qualifier and no split by type. Both recorded in the header; neither is asserted on a feature.
- ADA/UCG divergence NOT recorded: Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 could not be retrieved today (see unverified). Nothing from UCG is quoted, cited or asserted anywhere in the file; the header says so explicitly and names it as a CSO open item.
- GDM SCREENING — recorded on `uterus_large_for_dates` (UCG_2023_GDM.needsCsoInput + the importantNegative cited to ADA). ADA 2.32 screens EVERY pregnancy at 24–28 weeks; UCG §16.2.5 screens only on risk factors, printed under the heading 'Risk factors (and indication for screening)' — a list that includes polyhydramnios, foetus large for gestational age, glycosuria, BMI >35, age >40. The finding exists because UCG makes it an indication to screen; its importantNegative exists because under ADA a normal fundal height must never be read as a reason not to screen.
- WHEN AN OLDER ADULT'S TARGET IS RELAXED — recorded in full (both texts quoted) on ADA_S13_TARGETS.needsCsoInput. UCG §8.1.3 p 475 relaxes on CHRONOLOGICAL AGE: 'HbA1c <7% (7.5 % for elderly). Elderly people are at higher risk of hypoglycaemia … do not aim at very strict control.' ADA Table 13.2 relaxes on HEALTH STATUS and never mentions age — comorbidity count, ≥2 ADL impairments, cognitive impairment. Under UCG a robust 80-year-old is relaxed and a frail 60-year-old is not; under ADA the reverse. The three older-adult findings in this file are ADA's axes; UCG's rule needs only a date of birth.
- DKA/HHS DEFINITION (inherited, not re-litigated here): UCG contains no mention of DKA in pregnancy or euglycaemic DKA at all, so the euglycaemic-DKA content rests entirely on ADA S15 and the 2024 ADA/EASD consensus. Noted rather than presented as a disagreement.
- NO ADA/UCG DIVERGENCE IS RECORDED, and the header says why in full. The Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 PDF was requested from three hosts and fetched from none: differentiatedservicedelivery.org and guluhospital.net both exceed the fetch tool's 10 MB content-length ceiling, and library.health.go.ug fails TLS certificate verification. It is public and not paywalled — the tool could not carry it. The header directs the CSO to read UCG §8.1.4 and its upper-urinary-tract-infection section against this file before signing.
- Recorded divergence WITHIN the sources that were fetched: the ADA/EASD consensus gives only a warning about the DKA abdomen, while Umpierrez and Freire give the operational rule (investigate only without severe acidosis, or if the pain persists after ketoacidosis resolves). Both are carried, and the danger-sign gate encodes the second in this catalog's own ADA/EASD severity thresholds (bicarbonate 10 to under 18 mmol/L).
- THYROID: ADA rec 4.6 screens every person with type 1 diabetes for autoimmune thyroid disease and states routine thyroid screening is recommended for all of them (high prevalence, non-specific symptoms, insidious onset). UCG 2023 §§8.1.5–8.1.7 are symptom-driven entries that never mention diabetes, and the UCG diabetes chapter §8.1.3 asks for no thyroid test — so locally a person with type 1 diabetes is screened only if they look thyroidal. Both recorded in the file header and on UCG_2023_THYROID.needsCsoInput.
- THYROID EYE SIGNS: UCG gives 'Other causes of protruding eyes' its own line in the hyperthyroid differential, which is why the tier is moderate rather than strong; ISPAD adds that exophthalmos and lid lag 'may or may not be present'.
- COELIAC: ADA rec 4.7 and ISPAD both ask for case-finding. The retrieved full text of UCG 2023 was searched for 'coeliac', 'celiac' and 'gluten' and contains none — no Ugandan entry, no local pathway, and often no serology. Recorded on ISPAD_2022_COELIAC.needsCsoInput; both coeliac findings ship as ADA/ISPAD content in a Ugandan catalog.
- ADDISON: ADA/ISPAD frame it as autoimmune (APS-2 with type 1 diabetes and thyroid disease). UCG §8.1.1 leads with abrupt cessation of long-term steroids, then autoimmune destruction, then TB of the adrenals and HIV/AIDS, and puts diabetes mellitus in its differential. Both carried on the pigmentation finding — and the divergence is load-bearing, because UCG's leading cause is secondary insufficiency, which produces no pigmentation at all (that is the importantNegative's caveat).

## Where the evidence was too weak to assign a strength tier

These carry a qualitative tier and no numeric performance figure, or a declared gap. No
sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratio appears anywhere in the catalog unless it was fetched
from a primary source — there is exactly **one** numeric likelihood ratio in the whole corpus
(the 10 g monofilament, LR+ 4.56 / LR− 0.53, verified verbatim against PMC5651135), and its
citation records that the reference standard was nerve conduction studies, so it describes
_detecting_ neuropathy rather than _predicting_ ulceration.

- No likelihood ratios, sensitivities or specificities appear anywhere in the file. None of the retrieved sources published one for any of these findings, and none was invented to justify a strength tier.

- Umpierrez & Freire 2002 and Bersoff-Matcha 2019: ABSTRACTS ONLY. pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov returns a cookie wall to this tool and neither publisher full text was retrieved, so methods, case ascertainment and the definition of 'abdominal pain' used are unverified. Both citations say so in needsCsoInput. No sensitivity, specificity or likelihood ratio is claimed anywhere in the file.

## Deliberately not built

Each of these was considered and left out with a reason; the reasons are in the file comments.

- Proprioception / joint-position sense and Romberg's test — no retrieved guideline names them among the diabetic-neuropathy screening modalities (ADA names vibration for large fibre and stops there), so the field would have been documentation with no condition it could move.
- A separate temperature-sensation feature — ADA rec 12.18 says 'either temperature or pinprick', a choice not a pair, so the two are one field `small_fibre_sensation` with `small_fibre_modality` recording which was used. Two fields would have asked one question twice and then had to adjudicate a disagreement no source resolves.
- Erectile dysfunction — already captured as `sexual_dysfunction` in the autonomic symptom group, and it is a symptom with no bedside sign. Redefining it would give one finding two homes. Likewise early satiety (`gi_autonomic_symptoms`), postural dizziness and bladder symptoms.
- Peripheral dryness/cracking of the skin — ADA's third named sign of autonomic neuropathy, already `peripheral_dry_skin` in general-exam.ts.
- `postural_hypotension`, `lops`, `lops_ipswich` — not redefined, as instructed. `postural_pulse_response` is added beside the drop rather than replacing it, because ADA's neurogenic pattern is a drop 'without an appropriate increase in heart rate' and a boolean drop cannot express that.
- `importantNegative` on `monofilament_10g` refuting diabetic_peripheral_neuropathy — pooled sensitivity is 0.53, so a normal filament is a poor rule-out; the negative refutes charcot_neuroarthropathy only, with the caveat spelling that out. `vibration_128hz`, `resting_tachycardia` and `succussion_splash` carry no importantNegative at all, each for a stated reason.
- ageBands on every feature — none is age-specific in any retrieved source. The known confound (absent ankle jerks in older people without diabetes) is carried in the discrimination's rationale rather than as a band that would hide the field.
- Necrobiosis lipoidica, diabetic dermopathy, granuloma annulare, bullosis diabeticorum, scleredema, pruritus — already options of `diabetes_skin_lesions` in general-exam.ts. A second discriminating home would give each lesion two answers that can disagree.
- Eruptive xanthomata — the one dermatosis with a deadline, and the one that could not be fixed from this file. `urgency` and `discriminates` attach to a FEATURE, not to an option of a multi-select, and conditions.ts models no hypertriglyceridaemia for a discrimination to name. Closing it needs a condition entry, a fasting-triglyceride feature and the option promoted to a finding — none of them this file's to write. Written up in the header as an open CSO item.
- Xanthelasma — same gap without the deadline: no condition in conditions.ts for it to argue for, so under the parsimony rule it cannot be written.
- Candidal intertrigo — `skin_infection` already offers 'Fungal — intertrigo or candidal' with a site. Only the genital site is split out, because it is a first presentation rather than a complication and nobody looks unless asked.
- Insulin oedema — `peripheral_oedema` (graded, with extent) already owns the finding, no rankable condition corresponds to it, and no guideline retrieved for this build states a bedside definition. A field whose whole content is an attribution the examination cannot make is worse than none.
- Vitiligo and mucocutaneous hyperpigmentation — skin to look at, but they are markers for the autoimmune examination (exam-autoimmune.ts). Two authors each assuming the other had them is how a finding acquires two homes.
- Needle and syringe reuse — the commonest injection-technique problem in the setting, left out because no retrieved guideline ties it to anything this catalog ranks; it is a supply and education problem, and citing it to an invented source would be worse.
- Lipohypertrophy itself, acanthosis nigricans, lipodystrophy, skin_infection, peripheral_dry_skin — not redefined, per the brief. gx_injection therefore carries the two things `lipodystrophy` does not record: why the lump is there, and whether insulin is still going into it.
- A second AVPU/ACVPU. `avpu` (symptoms) and `mental_status` (acute) are referenced, never redefined — a second place to answer either is a second answer to disagree with. The one letter AVPU lacks, the C, is `disorientation`.
- A second focal-deficit screen. `focal_neurological_deficit` (symptoms) is WHO PEN-H's screen and already fires red; `lateralising_neurological_signs` is the elicited examination and deliberately carries NO danger sign — two reds for one problem teach people to ignore the first.
- GCS E/V/M as three fields. The total is what every protocol and every handover records; the components are demanded in `elicitation` instead, since no rule in this catalog reads them.
- A separate cranial-nerve-palsy finding. The orbit, eye movements and fundus are the eye segment's (ey_external / ey_fundus); a palsy found while comparing the two sides is recorded as a lateralising sign. Avoids giving one finding two homes.
- Tone, reflexes, plantars, gait, Romberg, neck stiffness — documentation, or arguing only for conditions outside the catalog. The cited sensory examination (monofilament, 128-Hz fork) belongs to the foot/neuropathy segments.
- Cognitive screening in older adults (ADA Section 13) — belongs to the age-specific segment, not to a conscious-level examination.
- importantNegative on `cerebral_oedema_late_signs` and on `lateralising_neurological_signs`. Late signs are by construction absent in the patient who most needs treating (BSPED: papilloedema is a late sign), and a symmetric examination does not argue usefully against hypoglycaemia or DKA. Claiming either refutation would have been an invention.
- Neonate / infant / child bands. BSPED and ISPAD cover under-18s; only `adolescent` is written (ADR 0083). `conscious_level_falling` is deliberately UNBANDED because JBDS states it for adults too.
- postural_hypotension — NOT redefined; it already exists in general-exam.ts (another author's file) as the boolean with the 20/10 mmHg label. I added the readings that establish it and the pulse response that interprets it, and gated the latter with showWhen on that code. ADA's definition retrieved here also closes the needsCsoInput gap that file declares on those numbers (its owner can lift it).
- Resting tachycardia >100 bpm (advanced CAN) and fever >38 °C / hypothermia <36 °C (IWGDF/IDSA grade 4) — deliberately NOT written as features. They are thresholds on `pulse` and `temperature`, which already exist; ADR 0083 puts numeric discrimination in a rule's Criterion, not in a second feature beside the measurement.
- Cushing response (falling pulse, rising BP, dropping conscious level in DKA) — left out. It is inseparable from conscious level, which is recorded once in the neurological examination; writing it here would give it two homes and risk a code collision with the CNS author.
- dangerSign on prolonged_capillary_refill — left out. The only action the retrieved sources give for a shocked patient is a fluid volume, and a volume is a dose this engine does not carry. Urgency `immediate` carries the same message on the orthogonal axis.
- importantNegative on prolonged_capillary_refill — deliberately absent. A brisk refill refutes SHOCK, which is not a rankable condition here, and the ADA/EASD consensus says patients can maintain haemodynamic stability despite severe hypovolaemia.
- Pulse rhythm / atrial fibrillation, narrow pulse pressure, BP in both arms — no rankable condition in conditions.ts, so nothing they could discriminate.
- Paediatric BP percentiles, BMI-for-age, Tanner staging, and the child/infant/neonate vital bands — deferred per the brief and ADR 0083.
- A published likelihood ratio anywhere — none was retrieved for these signs, so lrPositive/lrNegative are omitted and the qualitative tier stands alone.
- FRACTURE / BONE HEALTH — the one musculoskeletal topic ADA actually writes recommendations about (4.8, 4.11, 4.12). conditions.ts models no osteoporosis or fragility fracture, so height loss, kyphosis or vertebral tenderness would have nothing to argue for and the validator would reject them. Needs a condition entry plus a fracture-risk model, neither of which this file owns.
- GOUT, DIFFUSE IDIOPATHIC SKELETAL HYPEROSTOSIS, DIABETIC MYONECROSIS — all appear in the same prevalence surveys used here. None is in conditions.ts, DISH is radiological rather than bedside, and myonecrosis's real differential (DVT, pyomyositis, necrotising fasciitis) lies wholly outside this catalog. Inventing a weak link to justify listing them would have been padding; they go in `msk_exam_other`.
- THE PRAYER SIGN / CHEIROARTHROPATHY — not redefined. `limited_joint_mobility` in general-exam.ts already carries it with synonyms and backfilled discriminates. (Note for the CSO, not written into the file: Paul A, Gnanamoorthy K, Cureus 2023;15(3):e36701, PMC10129041, was fetched in full and would strengthen that EXISTING feature — 46/251 (18.3%) with cheiroarthropathy, retinopathy 35.7% vs 9.6%, nephropathy 26.8% vs 13%, neuropathy 34.9% vs 14.9%. It belongs to whoever owns general-exam.ts, not to me.)
- CHARCOT NEUROARTHROPATHY — a musculoskeletal diagnosis, but exam-foot.ts owns it end to end. PROXIMAL WASTING / DIABETIC AMYOTROPHY — already reached through `neuropathy_proximal_leg_pain`.
- NO `importantNegative` anywhere in the file. Each of these signs sits at roughly one in six of an unselected diabetes clinic (CTS 15–25% and flexor tenosynovitis ~a fifth in Chammas; Dupuytren 14% in Arkkila's type 2 arm), so a recorded absence carries almost no information and writing one would assert evidence the numbers do not support.
- NO `urgency` anywhere — none of these carries a deadline, and a clock on a painless hand sign would spend the alarm the foot needs. NO `ageBands` — every source studied adults but none establishes an age below which the signs do not occur (Arkkila's type 1 cohort mean age 33 ± 10), so banding would be a guess that hides the finding from the adolescent who has it.
- DUPUYTREN'S ASSOCIATIONS WITH RETINOPATHY AND NEUROPATHY — deliberately NOT written, although the chi-squared results are quoted in the citation. Arkkila's own logistic regression removed every association except macroalbuminuria in type 2 once age and duration were controlled; carrying an association the authors nullified would be quoting the paper against itself. Dupuytren therefore has exactly one discrimination.
- Visual symptoms (visual_symptoms, visual_pattern, visual_laterality, visual_eye, visual_loss_recovered, visual_eye_pain, last_retinal_exam) — already in the symptoms section with their red/amber flags; a second place to answer them is a second answer to disagree with.
- Papilloedema — already an option on cerebral_oedema_late_signs in exam-cns.ts, where BSPED puts it and labels it a late sign.
- Vitreous haemorrhage as its own finding — it is inside the ICDR proliferative level, and when dense enough to matter it is the reason the fundus cannot be graded, which fundus_view_obscured records with ICO's referral attached.
- Rubeosis iridis / neovascular glaucoma — NICE names it and the symptoms file already cites that, but no source retrieved for THIS file describes how to elicit it or what it obliges. A finding whose action cannot be sourced was not carried.
- Intraocular pressure, colour vision, visual fields, slit-lamp findings — not obtainable at the bedside this catalog targets.
- The 4-2-1 severe-NPDR components as four separate fields — they are the definition of a level on a scale the catalog carries whole; separate capture would create a second place for the grade to be decided. They live in the elicitation.
- Per-eye retinopathy grading as two parallel select fields — ADA 12.9 fires on the person, not the side, and a pair would fire the same amber twice for one problem. The worse eye is graded and the fellow eye is a property (which also makes the property gate itself correctly, since a 'none' worse eye means both eyes are clear).
- A pinhole feature — ICO's pinhole instruction is an elicitation step on the acuity measurement, not a separate finding.
- ageBands on anything — a retina looks the same at fifteen and at seventy; malignant otitis externa's age restriction already lives on the CONDITION in conditions.ts, so banding the finding would only stop an adolescent's ear being examined.
- requires: 'imaging.fundus' on anything — every retinal finding is defined by the scale as 'observable upon dilated ophthalmoscopy'; gating them would delete the eye examination from every site with an ophthalmoscope and no camera. The camera's real advantage (3-D view of macular thickening) is carried as the mandatory caveat on the maculopathy negative instead.
- FALLS — deliberately NOT written. `falls_last_year` already exists as a property of `unsteadiness_or_falls` in complication-symptoms.ts. It IS a real gap (gated behind `foot_symptom_screen`, so an older adult with no foot complaint is never asked, while ADA Table 13.1 screens every older adult), but the fix is to ungate the existing field in that author's file, not to add a second count of the same falls. Flagged in the file header for the CSO handover.
- POLYPHARMACY — omitted. ADA 13.2 screens for it, but the drugs that cause the harm are already captured by name and class (`glucose_lowering_therapy`, `sulfonylurea_agent`, `new_or_changed_drug`, `drug_change_non_diabetes`); a tablet count changes no differential those four do not. therapy-flags.ts is where a drug-plus-state rule belongs.
- COMORBIDITY COUNT (ADA Table 13.2's third axis, ≥3 chronic illnesses) — omitted. The problem list already holds them; a field asking the clinician to re-count would be a second copy that can disagree.
- PUBERTAL STAGE / prepubertal-at-onset — omitted. ISPAD makes it a type 1 feature, but inside the adolescent band (10–19) almost everyone is peri- or post-pubertal, so it does its work in the `child` band, which ADR 0083 defers. Banding it to a stage nothing renders would be writing it for nobody.
- A SECOND VOMITING HISTORY IN PREGNANCY — omitted. `vomiting`, `vomiting_duration`, `vomiting_tolerating_fluids` exist. What is stage-specific is what happens when a pregnant person cannot eat, which is one finding (`pregnancy_reduced_oral_intake`), not a duplicate symptom history.
- HYPOGLYCAEMIA UNAWARENESS IN PREGNANCY — omitted as a feature. ADA S15 records an altered counterregulatory response in pregnancy that may decrease awareness; that is a CAVEAT belonging on `hypo_autonomic_warning` in exam-cns.ts (whose importantNegative currently reads absent warning through long-standing type 1, recurrent lows and beta-blockade). Another author's feature, left alone; recorded in my header.
- THE PREGNANCY BLOOD-PRESSURE THRESHOLD (ADA 15.24: initiate/titrate at <140/90, deintensify below 90/60) — omitted, and this is a schema finding worth escalating: it CANNOT be expressed as an `ageRefRange` because pregnancy is a STATE, not an `AgeBand`. It belongs in an InterpretationTable with `when: { feature: "pregnant", op: "present" }` — interpretation.ts's to write.
- RETINOPATHY IN PREGNANCY (ADA 15.7, dilated exam first trimester and each trimester) — omitted. A screening INTERVAL on a complication, not a finding; `last_retinal_exam` already records when the eye was last looked at.
- importantNegative on `pregnancy_reduced_oral_intake` — deliberately NOT declared. Eating normally refutes essentially nothing (SGLT2 inhibitors account for most euglycaemic DKA and need no fasting; pregnancy is a listed cause in its own right). A negative whose caveat would have to say 'this excludes nothing' is worse than none.
- A `hypoglycaemia_unawareness` discrimination on `cognitive_impairment` — considered and rejected. ADA says care partners matter because such patients 'may not be able to report hypoglycemia symptoms', which is failure to REPORT, not failure to PERCEIVE. Asserting the condition code would have gone beyond the source.
- Guarding, rebound and rigidity are NOT graded. They are a property of abdominal_tenderness carrying no discriminates, because no retrieved source establishes that they separate a DKA abdomen from a surgical one — the sources' own discriminators are the severity of the acidosis and the response to treatment. They earn one declared limb of the amber danger sign, and that limb is flagged in needsCsoInput as the catalog's own judgement rather than the consensus's.
- Emphysematous cholecystitis: named in the crepitus elicitation and the header with its StatPearls figures, but NOT modelled as a feature — conditions.ts holds no code for it, that file belongs to another author, and a discriminates naming an undefined condition is a build error by design.
- Ballotable/enlarged kidney and ascites were drafted and struck. ADA Section 11's list of features suggesting non-diabetic kidney disease is entirely laboratory and ophthalmic — there is no physical sign on it — so any discrimination they carried would have been invented. A palpable bladder, the one abdominal finding that changes a rising creatinine, already exists as bladder_distension in exam-neuropathy.ts.
- perineal_soft_tissue_infection is not redefined; abdominal_wall_crepitus is scoped to the wall, flank and loin, which the exam-skin finding does not reach. succussion_splash, bladder_distension, insulin_lipohypertrophy, splenomegaly and peripheral_oedema are left with their existing owners.
- No systemic-toxicity/SIRS feature was added — the vitals already carry temperature, pulse and respiratory rate, and foot_systemic_signs already exists for its own region.
- No dangerSign on renal_angle_tenderness, pneumaturia or abdominal_pain_unresolved_after_treatment: urgency already answers the deadline question, and a second siren for one problem is how the first stops being read.
- Alopecia areata — ISPAD gives it 'clinical diagnosis / symptom related' only; it argues at best weakly for type 1 and nothing follows from it that vitiligo does not already trigger, while tinea capitis and traction alopecia dominate the local differential.
- Pretibial myxoedema and thyroid acropachy — rare, and only ever confirm a diagnosis the eye signs and goitre already made.
- goitre / goitre_character — NOT redefined; the neck is palpated once in general-exam.ts, where the ADA/UCG goitre divergence already lives.
- Coeliac abdominal signs (distension, wasting, pale stool) — the abdomen belongs to exam-abdomen.ts and another author; nutritional_wasting and pallor already exist in the general survey.
- The slow-relaxing ankle jerk — universally taught, stated by none of the retrieved guidelines, so not offered as an option; ankle_reflexes already records the jerk itself.
- The pulse in both thyroid option lists (resting tachycardia, bradycardia) — already a vital with a band and a general-survey finding (resting_tachycardia); a third home would be a third answer.
- No 'None of these' option on any multi-select: the ranker reads a non-empty recorded multi-select as PRESENT, so a ticked 'none' would argue FOR the finding. Every finding here is a boolean with a multi-select property instead.
- importantNegative omitted on thyroid_eye_signs, thyrotoxic_peripheral_signs, aphthous_ulceration, vitiligo and dermatitis_herpetiformis — absence is genuinely uninformative there (most AITD in T1D is Hashimoto's with no eye signs; coeliac in T1D is usually silent; 93–99% of people with type 1 diabetes have no vitiligo). Only two important negatives are carried: hypothyroid_signs and addisonian_hyperpigmentation.
- No laboratory features (TSH, tTG-IgA, 21-hydroxylase antibody) — every finding here ends in a test that this catalog does not yet model; named as a gap in the header rather than faked with a bedside field.
- No likelihood ratios, sensitivities or specificities anywhere. ISPAD's 1–7% vs 0.2–1% vitiligo prevalence and StatPearls' 'occurs in almost all patients' are quoted as the sources' own wording and explicitly not asserted as test performance.
