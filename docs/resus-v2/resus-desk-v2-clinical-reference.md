## 1. What changes clinically

1. **New: the ALS arrest console** replaces the "ALS module to follow" stub. It runs the 2-minute loop, rhythm checks, shocks, drug timing, airway/access, reversible causes and special-circumstance modifiers.
2. **New: ROSC → post-ROSC bundle → survey loop in post-ROSC context**, with re-arrest and Utstein "sustained ROSC".
3. **New: unsuccessful CPR path**: termination review, CPR-not-attempted path, confirmation of death, after-death tasks, immediate debrief.
4. **New: close-out**: outcome, level-of-care suggestion, capability-aware destination, structured handover, boarding phase.
5. **Unchanged clinical logic, new plumbing:** the signed A–E engines keep their rules, and step state stays engine-inferred (worst finding wins; an unassessed step is never treated as safe). Their inputs now come from (a) one patient-level context entered once instead of the same toggle repeated per step, (b) one arrest state instead of five "No pulse" / "No signs of life" toggles, and (c) Measure findings derived automatically from recorded vitals. All three are new input logic and need sign-off (D18–D20). Derivation is deliberately narrow: adults only (the bands use adult cut-offs), fresh vitals only (the imported triage reading can be up to 24 h old), only codes whose signed catalog entry carries an explicit number, and it can only make a step's state worse, never turn Pending into Clear.
6. **Two safety fixes found in the current screens:**
   - **Vitals never feed the step state.** The live desk shows C = "Clear" beside SBP 70, MAP 63, shock index 1.86. From v2, a step's displayed state can never be better than what its vitals-derived bands imply (worst finding wins, as already signed).
   - **The anaphylaxis bar still offers IM adrenaline 500 µg during an arrest.** In arrest the bar must switch to "follow ALS (IV adrenaline 1 mg)". Cardiac arrest from any special cause starts on the standard ALS algorithm [RCUK-SC25, general recommendation]; RCUK's separate anaphylaxis guidance covers the detail.

## 2. Arrest recognition, declaration and the first minute

**Criterion (unchanged, CSO-signed):** unresponsive AND not breathing normally and/or no central pulse / no signs of life; agonal gasps are not normal breathing; apnoea with a pulse is ventilate-not-CPR.

**Highly monitored patients** [RCUK-ALS25]: a sudden fall in ETCO₂ may indicate arrest or very low output; consider compressions if systolic BP falls and stays below 50 mmHg despite interventions.

**Declaration is one tap.** It stamps arrest and CPR-start time (the existing `arrest_detected` and `cpr_started` events) and switches the desk to the console. The existing multi-sign confirm stays (responsive, breathing normally, central pulse: yes / no / unknown), as does the signed rule that apnoea with a pulse means bag-mask ventilation, not CPR. The per-step "No signs of life" (A, B) and "No pulse" (C, D, E) toggles become one patient-level arrest state set through that confirm (D20). A triage rapid-Red "no pulse / arrest" opens the desk straight into the console. Everything else is captured later, never before CPR:

| Utstein field (captured after, non-blocking)  | Default                       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| Time of collapse, if earlier than declaration | = declaration time            |
| Witnessed? Monitored at time of arrest?       | unknown                       |
| CPR started before declaration (time)         | = declaration time            |
| First documented rhythm                       | set by the first rhythm check |

**Treatment-limitation check.** If a treatment-limitation / not-for-CPR decision exists in this episode (or in the encounter record once live), the console shows it at declaration with who recorded it and when. It never suppresses CPR. Emergency care plans are recommendations; the final decision rests with the clinicians treating the patient at that moment unless a legally binding document exists [RCUK-ETH25].

**CPR-not-attempted path** (secondary control, reason mandatory): valid recorded treatment-limitation decision, or obvious irreversible death. Routes straight to confirmation of death (Section 9).

**Team:** arrest fires an arrest call on top of the opening mobilisation. The Family role gets a prompt: offer the family the choice to be present, with a trained team member assigned to support them [RCUK-ETH25].

## 3. The ALS loop: rules the arrest engine must implement

### 3.1 CPR quality and cycle timing

| Rule                                        | Value                                                                                                         | Source     |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Compression rate                            | 100–120 /min                                                                                                  | ERC-BLS25  |
| Compression depth                           | ≥ 5 cm, not more than 6 cm; full recoil; don't lean                                                           | ERC-BLS25  |
| Compressor rotation                         | every 2 min, at the rhythm-check pause                                                                        | AHA-BLS25  |
| Cycle                                       | single shock, then 2 min CPR; rhythm/pulse check every 2 min                                                  | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Rhythm identification                       | within 5 s                                                                                                    | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Peri-shock pause                            | aim < 5 s; charge during compressions                                                                         | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Pre-charging before rhythm check            | acceptable if it doesn't lengthen the pause                                                                   | RCUK-ALS25 |
| After shock                                 | resume compressions immediately                                                                               | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Early stop for rhythm/pulse check           | only on a combination of ROSC signs (consciousness, purposeful movement, arterial waveform, sharp ETCO₂ rise) | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Filtered-ECG defibrillator showing asystole | no need to pause compressions for the rhythm check                                                            | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Shock during manual compressions            | never (rescuer safety, even gloved)                                                                           | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Oxygen at shock                             | mask / cannulae / BVM ≥ 1 m from chest; SGA/ETT circuit stays connected                                       | RCUK-ALS25 |

### 3.2 Shockable (VF / pVT)

| Rule                                                     | Value                                                                                                                                                | Source     |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Shock                                                    | as early as possible, any amplitude (fine VF included)                                                                                               | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Adrenaline, first dose                                   | **1 mg IV after the 3rd shock**                                                                                                                      | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Amiodarone                                               | **300 mg IV after a total of 3 shocks**; **further 150 mg IV after a total of 5 shocks**, whether VF/pVT is sequential or intermittent               | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Lidocaine (alternative)                                  | **100 mg IV** if amiodarone unavailable or by local decision; **additional 50 mg** after 5 shocks                                                    | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Stacked shocks                                           | up to 3, only for VF/pVT in a witnessed, monitored arrest with a defibrillator immediately available; **count the stack as shock 1** for drug timing | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Refractory VF (continuous VF after 3 consecutive shocks) | consider escalating energy; consider **vector change** to antero-posterior pads, prepared at the next rhythm check                                   | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Dual sequential defibrillation                           | not recommended for routine use (RCUK)                                                                                                               | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Pad position                                             | antero-lateral first; lateral pad below the armpit, mid-axillary line; ICD/pacemaker: pad > 8 cm away                                                | RCUK-ALS25 |

**Energy** [RCUK-ALS25, AHA-ALS25]:

- Rectilinear or truncated-exponential biphasic: first shock **≥ 150 J**. Pulsed biphasic: first shock **130–150 J**. Escalate after a failed shock if the device allows.
- Energy settings unknown: **use the highest setting for every shock**. Standard energy in obesity.
- AHA: follow the manufacturer's setting; unknown → maximum.
- **MedSync encoding:** the default energy sequence comes from the bay's _registered defibrillator profile_ (waveform + manufacturer sequence, entered by biomedical engineering, CSO-approved). With no profile, the only rule shown is "highest available setting". No hard-coded joule number without a profile.

### 3.3 Non-shockable (PEA / asystole)

| Rule       | Value                                                        | Source     |
| ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| Adrenaline | **1 mg IV as soon as possible**                              | RCUK-ALS25 |
| Asystole   | check the ECG for P waves (more likely to respond to pacing) | RCUK-ALS25 |

### 3.4 Adrenaline repeat and rhythm changes

- **Repeat 1 mg every 3–5 min while ALS continues** [RCUK-ALS25]. Display as a window: opens at +3:00 from the last dose, overdue after +5:00. Optional cue: "every second loop" [ANZCOR-11.5].
- Shockable → non-shockable before the 3rd shock: adrenaline becomes due now (non-shockable rule).
- Shocks are counted per arrest; amiodarone eligibility uses the total, irrespective of interruptions [RCUK-ALS25].
- Adrenaline concentration for IV use in arrest is recorded (1:10,000 per RCUK-SC25 wording); the existing look-alike guard (1:1000 IM vs 1:10,000 IV) applies.

### 3.6 Drugs and fluids: what is and is not routine

- Fluids **only** if the arrest is caused by hypovolaemia; isotonic saline or balanced crystalloid [RCUK-ALS25].
- **Not routine:** calcium, sodium bicarbonate, corticosteroids [RCUK-ALS25]. They appear only inside the relevant reversible-cause tile (Section 4).
- CPR-induced consciousness (rare): small doses of sedative/analgesic per local protocol (e.g. fentanyl, ketamine, midazolam); never a neuromuscular blocker alone [RCUK-ALS25]. **Doses NOT ENCODED.** Controlled-drug witness rule applies.
- Thrombolysis for suspected or confirmed PE: consider CPR for **60–90 min** after administration [RCUK-ALS25, RCUK-SC25]. **Thrombolytic doses NOT ENCODED** (local protocol).

## 6. ROSC

**Recognition** [RCUK-ALS25]: a combination of consciousness, purposeful movement, arterial waveform or a sharp ETCO₂ rise justifies stopping for rhythm analysis and a pulse check. The ROSC record stores which signs were present.

**Definitions** (use for all reporting):

- **Any ROSC:** return of a palpable pulse / patient-generated blood pressure.
- **Sustained ROSC:** chest compressions not required for **20 consecutive minutes** with signs of circulation persisting [UTS-IH19]. "Time CPR stopped" = start of sustained ROSC, or termination [UTS-IH19].
- **Intermittent ROSC:** ROSC then re-arrest before 20 min; the same arrest attempt continues.
- ROSC > 20 min may be used to mark where one CPR attempt ends and a new one begins [UTS-IH97]. **Engine rule:** re-arrest after sustained ROSC opens a new arrest record (sequence + 1); cumulative episode drug totals stay visible for dose-safety checks.

**Console behaviour:** ROSC starts a visible 20-minute sustain timer and switches to post-ROSC mode. A prominent **Re-arrest** control returns to the console with counters intact. (Today the ⚠ Arrest control hides once declared and the episode stores a single arrest time; v2 adds an arrest number.)

## 7. Post-ROSC bundle (resus phase)

Post-resuscitation care starts **immediately after sustained ROSC, regardless of location** [RCUK-PRC25]. Here "sustained" means circulation that is holding, not the 20-minute Utstein reporting definition; the desk therefore shows the bundle from the moment ROSC is recorded (CSO to confirm this reading). Each item can be _done_, _not indicated_, or **not available here** (a distinct state; it feeds capability data).

## 8. Termination of resuscitation (unsuccessful CPR)

### 8.1 Principles

- **Team-based, holistic decision** weighing the patient's values and preferences and the combined prognostic picture: CPR duration, absence of reversible causes, no response to ALS [RCUK-ETH25].
- **TOR rules should not be used for in-hospital arrest** (insufficient evidence) [RCUK-ETH25]; nor as the sole strategy for stopping an IHCA attempt [RCUK-ALS25]. Organisations should set local criteria for their legal, organisational and cultural context [RCUK-ETH25].
- **Persistent-asystole criterion — the guidelines differ:**
  - ERC 2025 Ethics: TOR _may be considered_ with persistent asystole despite **20 min** of ALS, no reversible cause, and nothing else arguing against it [ERC-ETH25].
  - RCUK 2025 Ethics: persistent asystole from confirmation of arrest despite **at least 45 min** of ALS, same conditions [RCUK-ETH25].
  - → CSO decision **D1** (Section 14).
- **ETCO₂:** persistently low ETCO₂ is a strong marker that may aid the decision, never in isolation [RCUK-ETH25, RCUK-ALS25].
- **Not a basis for stopping:** cardiac ultrasound, blood gases, pupil reactivity [RCUK-ETH25].
- Carry it out **in a planned manner**; every team member gets a chance to contribute; **document reasons** (audited) [RCUK-ETH25].
- **Debrief immediately** after termination [RCUK-ETH25].
- Context, not rule: survival to discharge is possible after prolonged IHCA (> 1 h) though below 1% after 40 min in one large study [ERC-ALS25].

### 8.2 Flags that argue for continuing (shown on the review)

Hypothermia (rewarm before deciding); toxins / LAST (> 1 h); PE after thrombolysis (60–90 min clock); persistent or recurrent shockable rhythm; intermittent ROSC; rising ETCO₂; pregnancy with hysterotomy pending; a reversible cause being treated right now.

### 8.3 Desk flow

1. **Review** (anyone may open; leader decides): arrest duration, CPR duration, time in persistent asystole since confirmation, rhythm history, shocks, adrenaline doses, airway + capnography status, last three ETCO₂ values, **reversible causes not yet excluded (listed first, amber)**, continue-flags, any treatment-limitation decision.
2. **Team pause:** "Team asked, no objection" / "Objection raised" (free text); senior consulted (name, cadre, in person/phone); family present / informed / not reachable.
3. **Reasons** (structured multi-select + free text).
4. **Stop resuscitation at [time]** → Section 9.

- Cadre authority is **CSO decision D9**. Never a hard block: a non-authorised cadre records the decision with a senior co-sign pending.

## 9. Confirmation of death after cardiorespiratory arrest [AOMRC25]

1. Observe for **at least 5 minutes** [s5.13] for all three of:
   - **continuous unconsciousness**;
   - **continuous apnoea**: no visible chest movement and no breath sounds on auscultation;
   - **continuous absence of circulation**: no central pulse on palpation and no heart sounds on auscultation.

   In hospital, monitoring can support this: asystole on continuous ECG, no pulsatile arterial trace, no contractile activity on echo [AOMRC08].

2. **Any spontaneous return of circulatory, respiratory or neurological function** → a further 5-minute assessment from the next point of cardiorespiratory arrest [s5.16]. The desk offers "Signs of life — resume" to go back to the console or ROSC.
3. After the 5 minutes: confirm absent pupillary responses to light and no motor response to supraorbital pressure; soon after an arrest, also confirm absent corneal reflexes [s5.15].
4. **Time of death = when the clinician is satisfied that all the criteria are met** [s5.17]. Record the confirming clinician.

In the UK the confirmer can be any appropriately trained and competent professional, including doctors, registered nurses, advanced practitioners and paramedics [s5.7]. **Uganda:** who may confirm, the certification form, civil registration duties, and which deaths must be reported to police are legal questions → **CSO/legal decision D10**. The desk records the facts and leaves these as configured tasks.

## 10. After-death tasks (checklist; not clinical logic)

A separate record from the episode timeline, so it can be completed after the episode closes; the closed episode itself stays read-only (D16). Family informed (who, when, where; offered to see the patient; cultural and religious needs) · senior/consultant informed · **reportable-death screen** (per D10; if reportable, leave lines, tubes and devices in place) · last offices · property handover · certificate of cause of death (seam to the encounter record; not built) · mortuary transfer · **immediate team debrief** [RCUK-ETH25] · staff welfare check · Utstein data completed. Organ donation after cardiac arrest must follow local legal and ethical requirements [RCUK-PRC25, RCUK-ETH25]; hidden unless a hospital enables it.

## 11. Close-out, handover and level of care

### 11.1 Handover

Use a structured tool [RCUK-ALS25: structured communication for handover]. The desk drafts an **SBAR** from the record, editable before signing:

- **S:** who, trigger, current state and mode (stabilised / post-ROSC), treatment-limitation decision if any.
- **B:** AMPLE (allergies, medications, past history, last meal, events), weight if recorded.
- **A:** final A–E states with key findings; vitals trend + last set + NEWS2; arrest summary if any (first rhythm, shocks, drugs, time to ROSC, total CPR time, sustained or not); lines, tubes, devices.
- **R:** outstanding actions carried forward, targets (post-ROSC bundle), pending investigations, suggested vs chosen level of care, escort needs.

### 11.2 Levels of care [ICS-LOC21]

ICS levels describe the care a patient **needs**, not where they are. That is exactly how the recommender works: compute the need, then map it to what this hospital's units can deliver.

| Level                | Meaning (paraphrased)                                                                                                                                                       | ICS examples                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ward                 | needs met on an acute ward, including patients at risk of deterioration                                                                                                     | DKA improving without organ support; ED hypotension that responded to fluids                                                                                    |
| **L1 Enhanced care** | more observation/intervention incl. basic support for a single organ; step-down; outreach-dependent                                                                         | vasopressor (peripheral/central) but stable; NIV/CPAP for single-organ failure; close physiological monitoring after major surgery (e.g. arterial line in situ) |
| **L2**               | basic support for ≥ 2 organs, or one organ at an advanced level (other than advanced respiratory); L1 needs + severe agitation; major uncorrected physiological abnormality | NIV/CPAP + vasopressor; RRT outside a renal setting; L1 needs + hyperactive delirium                                                                            |
| **L3**               | advanced respiratory support alone; ≥ 2 organs at an advanced level; chronic organ impairment + acute failure of another; L2 needs + delirium                               | mechanical ventilation; RRT + vasopressor or respiratory support; NIV/CPAP + vasopressor with agitation or at risk of escalation                                |

### 11.3 NEWS2 response [RCP-NEWS2]

- **≥ 7:** emergency response; continuous monitoring; assessment by a critical-care-competent team including advanced airway skills; **consider transfer to level 2 or 3 care**.
- **5–6:** urgent response threshold; at least hourly observations.
- **A 3 in any single parameter:** at least hourly observations; the nurse informs the medical team, who review and decide whether to escalate.
- 1–4: 4–6-hourly. 0: 12-hourly.
- Reuse the triage engine's verified NEWS2 implementation; do not re-implement scoring.

**Additional destinations flagged alongside the level:** cath lab (STEMI / suspected coronary occlusion, Section 7); theatre (uncontrolled haemorrhage, surgical source); obstetric theatre / labour ward (pregnancy-related cause); CT before the unit (head injury, suspected non-coronary arrest cause). Condition-specific triggers not listed here (severe DKA markers, eclampsia on magnesium, severe TBI) are **NOT ENCODED** until the CSO verifies thresholds.

**Capability mapping.** Each unit declares what it can deliver (continuous ECG, arterial line, vasopressors, NIV/CPAP, invasive ventilation, RRT; adult/paediatric). The recommender converts the level into required capabilities and shows which units qualify. Most Ugandan hospitals have no separate enhanced-care area, so an L1 need will usually match only an HDU (the unit that declares vasopressor capability); the mapping handles this without special cases. None qualifies → **transfer to a higher-level facility** is suggested. Choosing a unit below the suggestion requires a reason: _no bed_, _ceiling of care agreed_, _family declined_, _transfer arranged_, _clinical judgement_ (free text). This builds a record of unmet critical-care need.

### 11.5 Boarding

When a destination is decided but the patient cannot leave yet (no bed), the episode enters **boarding**: monitoring frequency follows the NEWS2 response; deterioration → "Re-activate resus" (back to the survey, same episode). Boarding time is measured from disposition decision to leaving resus.

## 12. Data, quality and the Ugandan context

### 12.1 Utstein IHCA core data [UTS-IH19]

Capture at close-out for every arrest (snapshot, immutable): monitoring in place at arrest; witnessed; time of arrest; time CPR started; first documented rhythm; defibrillator applied / shocks given and time of first shock; time of first adrenaline; airway used; ECPR (capability-gated); any ROSC; sustained ROSC; time CPR stopped; reason CPR stopped (survived event vs died); a prior not-for-CPR decision if one existed. Survival to discharge / 30 days and neurological outcome link later through the inpatient module. **Verify the full core list against the 2019 paper at build.**

### 12.2 Data-driven debrief

Debriefing should be data-driven and performance-focused [RCUK-ALS25]. The desk pre-computes: time to first shock; time to first adrenaline; adrenaline interval adherence (3–5 min window); rhythm-check interval adherence; time to advanced airway and capnography use; reversible causes addressed; compressor rotation.

### 12.3 Why this matters in Uganda

- At Mulago, cardiac arrest occurred in 2.3% of admissions (190 of 8,131, Feb–Apr 2014), with ROSC in 7.4% [UG-IHCA]. Over 840 Ugandan health workers were ALS/ACLS-trained by Dec 2023, yet real-world use of those skills had not been assessed [UG-ALS]. A console that prompts timing and reversible causes is a cognitive aid for exactly this gap.
- Critical care is scarce: 55 functional ICU beds (1.3 per million) in the national survey, most in the central region; non-ventilated ICU beds were used as HDU because separate HDUs were lacking [UG-ICU]. ICU services have since been extended to 14 regional referral hospitals, with workforce the binding constraint [UG-SCALE]. → capability-aware disposition, "not available here" states, and unmet-need data are core, not extras.
- ALS may need adapting to local resources [ERC-ALS25; ILCOR-LRS]. → the bay equipment profile (defibrillator type, capnography, ultrasound, mechanical CPR, ECPR) switches prompts on and off honestly.
- Formulary: amiodarone vs lidocaine, calcium chloride vs gluconate availability varies. → hospital setting for the antiarrhythmic (D5); gluconate substitute pending CSO (Section 4). Cross-check with the UCG/EMHSLU revision.
