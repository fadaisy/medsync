## 1. The relation model

### 1.1 Four stored relations, one place and one versioning rule

The six requirements need fewer stored relation types than they name, because two of them are places on the board and one is a versioning rule.

| Requirement                          | How it is held                                            | Stored as                                                        |
| ------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| R1 Secondary to, and complication of | One causal relation with a label                          | `due_to`, label `secondary`, `complication` or `precipitated_by` |
| R2 Risk factors                      | A derived line; stored only if the clinician includes it  | `risk_factor_for` (optional)                                     |
| R3 Subtypes                          | A new version of the same condition                       | No relation: a version with `refined_from`                       |
| R4 Co-existing                       | A place on the board                                      | No relation (that is the point)                                  |
| R5 Background                        | A place on the board; a link when it is active this visit | `episode_of`                                                     |
| goes_with pairs (already signed)     | A non-causal clinical link                                | `associated_with`                                                |

Why merge complication and secondary into one `due_to` (D-REL-03): both say "A is caused by B". They map to the same ICD-11 axis and the same FHIR extension, and the death-certificate chain needs one kind of arrow, not two that mean the same. The label carries the wording. The catalogue sets the label; where a pair is already in the signed complication list, the label is `complication` whatever else says so (the builder relabelled 124 cause pairs this way). The clinician can change it.

`precipitated_by` is kept separate from `secondary` because the wording matters clinically: pneumonia does not cause diabetes; it precipitates an episode (DKA) of it.

Reading direction for every relation is "condition — relation — related condition":

- _Anaemia_ `due_to` _malaria_ (label `secondary`)
- _DKA_ `due_to` _type 1 diabetes_ (label `complication`) and _DKA_ `due_to` _pneumonia_ (label `precipitated_by`)
- _Type 2 diabetes_ `risk_factor_for` _NSTEMI_
- _Hypertension, this visit_ `episode_of` _hypertension, problem list_
- _Type 2 diabetes_ `associated_with` _hypertension_ (symmetric; stored once)

### 1.2 Each relation in detail

**`due_to`** (R1)

- _Direction and who creates it._ From the effect to the cause. Any clinician who can edit the encounter and can view the classes of both conditions. Created only by the clinician; the catalogue only offers.
- _What the clinician sees._ On the higher-placed of the two diagnoses (see §2.3): "secondary to malaria", "complication of known type 1 diabetes", "precipitated by pneumonia", with the label as a word, never colour alone.
- _Note wording._
  - "Anaemia, severe (Hb 62 g/L), secondary to severe malaria and to hookworm infection."
  - "Diabetic ketoacidosis, complication of known type 1 diabetes; precipitated by community-acquired pneumonia and by missed insulin."
  - Chains read in order: "Acute kidney injury, KDIGO stage 2, secondary to sepsis, secondary to acute pyelonephritis."
  - When the child's own name already names the parent (diabetic retinopathy under diabetes), the note does not repeat "complication of diabetes".
- _ICD-11._ The effect's stem, post-coordinated with **has causing condition** pointing to the cause's stem, where the MMS sanctions that axis on the stem. Where it is not sanctioned, both stems are coded separately (or as a cluster) and the link lives only in MedSync's record. `precipitated_by` has no ICD-11 axis; both are coded separately. **Has manifestation** applies only where the MMS models the child as a manifestation of the parent. Every pairing is "to verify" against the MMS API.
- _FHIR._ On the effect's Condition, the extension `condition-dueTo` with `valueReference` to the cause's Condition, plus a MedSync extension carrying the label. `condition-occurredFollowing` is an alternative for `precipitated_by` if a downstream system expects it. Extension URLs are "to verify" against the R4 registry.
- _HMIS and DHIS2._ Each diagnosis is counted on its own line (D-REL-07); the relation does not suppress either count. At death, the `due_to` chain _proposes_ the Part I sequence of the medical certificate of cause of death (a: effect; b: its cause; and so on, up to d), for the certifying doctor to confirm, shorten or reorder. The underlying cause is the last line the doctor keeps. Nothing is written to the certificate automatically.
- _When either end changes:_
  - _The cause is refined_ (malaria uncomplicated to severe). The relation points at the logical condition, so it follows the new version; the note text updates.
  - _The cause is refuted or entered in error._ The system retires the relation with reason "cause removed" (audited, source `system_parent_removed`). The effect stays, moves up to the place its parent held if it was linked under it, and shows the marker "Cause removed: review", which the clinician must acknowledge before signing. The effect is never deleted and never silently re-linked (vignette DXV-13).
  - _The effect is entered in error._ The relation is retired with it.
  - _Final and co-signature._ The relation is part of the effect's assertion. A junior's Final on the effect needs a senior co-signature, and that co-signature covers its relations. A senior who changes a relation creates a new version, and the co-signature applies to that (DXV-14).
- _Restricted classes._ The relation carries every restricted class of its two ends. A viewer without one of those classes does not receive the relation row at all (row-level security, not just UI hiding). That viewer sees the unrestricted end alone, with no "secondary to…" and no placeholder (D-REL-09; DXV-05, DXV-19). A placeholder would disclose that a restricted condition exists.
- _Rules the database enforces._
  - No relation on a differential (D-REL-08).
  - No self-relation and no cycle.
  - At most 4 levels of `due_to`, matching certificate lines a to d.
  - Several causes per effect are allowed, ordered by `position` (D-REL-12).

**`associated_with`** (the signed goes_with pairs)

- _Direction._ Symmetric; stored once with the lower id first.
- _Who creates it._ As for `due_to`.
- _What is seen and written._ "Type 2 diabetes, with hypertension."
- _ICD-11._ The **associated with** axis where the MMS sanctions it; otherwise the conditions are coded separately.
- _FHIR._ `condition-related`.
- _HMIS._ Each counted on its own.
- _Behaviour and restricted classes._ As for `due_to`, but removing either end simply retires the relation, with no "review" marker, because no causal claim is lost.
- _Its purpose._ It keeps today's "With:" behaviour for goes_with pairs honest: it does not pretend a cause, and it does not drop the link.

**`risk_factor_for`** (R2)

- _What it is._ Not a cause. The desk _derives_ one line under the outcome from three places: the problem list, this visit's diagnoses and the social history (tobacco, alcohol, inactivity). For example: "Risk factors on record: type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidaemia; current tobacco use."
- _When it is stored._ Only if the clinician ticks "include in note" (D-REL-04). Then one `risk_factor_for` row per factor that is a condition; exposures stay in the social history.
- _Not named twice._ If a factor is already linked as this outcome's cause, the line leaves it out. The risk-factor file lists 12 pairs that can be both, for example hypertension with heart failure.
- _Coding and counting._ Not coded as a relation in ICD-11. In FHIR, `condition-related` with a MedSync relation-type code `risk-factor`. Never counted in HMIS. Never adds anything to the problem list.
- _Restricted classes._ The line carries the restricted classes of the outcome and of every factor it names (DXV-10: a TB outcome makes the whole line TB-restricted).

**`episode_of`** (R5, when a known condition is active this visit)

- _What it is._ The clinician taps "Active this visit" on a background chip. This creates a this-visit diagnosis (`encounter_diagnosis`) linked `episode_of` the problem-list condition.
- _What it avoids._ No duplicate problem-list entry is ever created, and the problem-list entry is not re-diagnosed.
- _What the note says._ "Hypertension, known since 2019: not controlled at this visit, BP 172/104…" (DXV-12).
- _FHIR._ The idiomatic export needs no new Condition: `Encounter.diagnosis.condition` references the problem-list Condition, with its rank. The this-visit row exists in MedSync so that the visit can carry its own status, stage and supports.
- _HMIS._ Counted as a revisit, not a new case, where the return separates the two.
- _If the background condition itself changes_ (for example HIV refined to advanced HIV disease), that is a new version of the problem-list condition, made through this link (DXV-05).

### 1.3 What a relation is never

- Never created by the system. The one exception is retiring a relation whose end was removed, which is recorded as a system action.
- Never inferred from the catalogue without a clinician's action.
- Never a source of alarms. Interrupts stay in the triage and pack acute lanes, once per event; a relation adds no red.
- Never shown by colour alone: every relation has a word.

## 2. The board, redesigned for R1 to R5

### 2.1 The places

| Place                                 | Means                                                                                        | What can move in                                                                                   | What can move out                                                                                                                  | Signing                                                                   | Problem list on signing                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Leading** (one)                     | This visit's primary diagnosis                                                               | Any working or final diagnosis; a promoted differential; a background chip via "Active this visit" | Swap with a co-existing diagnosis (one action); demote to differential with a reason                                               | As today; a junior's Final needs co-signature, which covers its relations | Added if the catalogue's `problem_list` behaviour says so, with the clinician's confirmation |
| **Also this visit: co-existing** (R4) | Managed this visit, not caused by and not causing the leading diagnosis                      | Any working or final diagnosis                                                                     | Link it under another diagnosis (it becomes "linked"); swap to leading                                                             | As leading                                                                | As leading                                                                                   |
| **Linked** (under a diagnosis)        | Shown under the diagnosis it relates to: complication, secondary, precipitant, or associated | A co-existing diagnosis dropped onto another; a catalogue candidate from "Add a linked diagnosis"  | Unlink (back to co-existing); re-link to another diagnosis                                                                         | Its own status; co-signature covers its relation                          | As leading; nested under its parent where both are on the list                               |
| **Differential**                      | Alternatives to the leading diagnosis, ranked Likely, Possible or Unlikely                   | As today                                                                                           | Promote; mark Unlikely with a reason (must-not-miss stays pinned until then)                                                       | Not signed as diagnoses                                                   | Never                                                                                        |

### 2.2 The ambiguities, resolved

- **Is a co-existing diagnosis also primary?** No. One primary per visit stays (D-REL-01). Co-existing diagnoses have `rank = secondary` but no parent, sit at the same visual weight as the leading card, and are counted on their own line. Swapping which one leads is a single action. ICD-11 morbidity rules and inpatient returns need one main condition.
- **Can a differential be "secondary to" something?** No (D-REL-08). A causal arrow asserts more than either diagnosis alone, so both ends must be working or final. The suspected cause goes in the differential's reasoning; on promotion the desk offers the catalogued link (DXV-16). The database refuses a relation where either end's verification is `differential` or `refuted`.
- **Can a background condition be the parent of a complication at this visit?** Yes, and this is why relations must live in the record (D-REL-02): DKA now, `due_to` known type 1 diabetes on the problem list (DXV-04). The link targets the problem-list condition directly; the diabetes is not re-diagnosed.
- **What happens when a background condition is confirmed active this visit?** "Active this visit" creates an `episode_of` diagnosis in leading or co-existing; the problem list keeps one entry (DXV-12). If the clinician refines it (HIV to advanced HIV disease), the problem-list condition gets the new version through that link (DXV-05).

### 2.3 Where a relation is shown, and in what order the note writes

- **Shown once.** A relation appears once, on whichever of its two diagnoses sits higher: leading, then co-existing, then linked, then background. It is phrased from that side.
  - If the cause is higher, it reads as a child line under the cause: "Rheumatic heart disease — Heart failure (LVEF 55%), complication of rheumatic heart disease".
  - If the effect is higher, it reads as a clause on the effect: "Diabetic ketoacidosis, complication of known type 1 diabetes; precipitated by pneumonia".
  - The clinician never sees the same arrow twice.
- **The note's Impression is written in this order:**
  1. the leading diagnosis, with its status, stage and qualifiers, then its relation clauses, then its linked children, indented;
  2. each co-existing diagnosis, in board order, in the same form;
  3. the derived risk-factor line, if included, under the diagnosis it belongs to;
  4. "Considered and unlikely" or "Ruled out", for refuted must-not-miss differentials and for replaced diagnoses;
  5. "Known conditions", listing the background items not already named above.
- **Restricted ends drop out per viewer** with their clauses, leaving grammatical sentences: no dangling "secondary to".

### 2.4 The existing associations section

The 8 October build keeps working. "Add an association" becomes **"Add a linked diagnosis"** and lists candidates from three sources:

- the signed complications of the diagnosis (offered as `due_to`, label complication);
- the cause catalogue, for diagnoses this one can cause (offered as `due_to`, with the label);
- the goes_with pairs (offered as `associated_with`).

The "Add findings to" switch, move up and down, and flag stay as they are. `meta[parent].with` and `flagged` become relation rows (§5).

## 3. Refinement (R3)

- **Refine means a new version of the same condition** (D-REL-05). The logical condition id stays; the term, the code and possibly the kind change. History keeps every earlier term with who refined it, when, and the deciding results. The board and note show the current term with "refined from …". Relations follow automatically because they point at the logical condition.
- **A change of identity is Replace, not Refine.** If ACS turns out to be pericarditis, the clinician uses **Replace**. That refutes ACS with a reason and creates Acute pericarditis as a new condition, in one action. The note keeps "Ruled out: acute coronary syndrome (ECG widespread ST-elevation with PR depression; troponin not raised at 0 h and 3 h)" (DXV-11). The subtype file lists, for ACS, NSTE-ACS, stroke and pneumonia, the `alternatives_if_ruled_out` that the desk offers under Replace. These are never refinements.
- **Evidence carries over.** Supports and against-findings stay with the condition. The deciding results are appended as "decided by: troponin 10:10 and 13:10 (rise)". Nothing is dropped on refinement.
- **How the desk prompts.**
  - The broad diagnosis shows a line from its `decided_by` tests and the placed orders, each in words: "Type to be decided by: ECG (done 10:05) · troponin (ordered, result pending)".
  - When a recorded result meets a subtype's criteria, the desk offers "Refine to NSTEMI", naming the criterion and its source. It never refines by itself.
  - A blank or "not done" result never refines.
  - A single value never satisfies a criterion that needs a series: an NSTEMI needs a rise or fall.
- **Two-step refinements are normal.** ACS becomes STEMI or NSTE-ACS from the ECG; NSTE-ACS becomes NSTEMI or unstable angina from serial troponin (ESC 2023, sections 2.1 and 3.2). This is why `dx.nste_acs` is proposed: without it, ACS would have to jump to a final type before the troponin decides.
- **Note before and after.** Each parent in the subtype file has `note_before` and `note_after`, for example "Acute coronary syndrome, type to be decided by ECG and troponin" becoming "Non-ST-elevation myocardial infarction (refined from acute coronary syndrome after ECG and troponin rise)".
- **Severity is not refinement** (D-REL-14). Anaemia severity (WHO 2024), CKD G and A categories (KDIGO 2024), AKI stage (KDIGO 2012), CURB-65 and BP grade are stages in `conditions.stage`, recomputed as results change, written in brackets in the note. A blank input makes the stage "insufficient", never the lowest band: no baseline creatinine means no AKI stage (DXV-13), and a woman who could be pregnant with unknown pregnancy status gets no anaemia severity, as in the diabetes pack.
- **Placeholders are refined when explained** (D-REL-15). "Fever, cause not yet known" becomes "Malaria, uncomplicated" when the RDT is positive (DXV-17). "Anaemia, cause not yet known" becomes "Anaemia" once a cause is linked, so the note does not say "cause not yet known… secondary to malaria".
- **Refinement can be suppressed by a relation.** Heart failure linked to valve disease is not offered the LVEF classes; the LVEF is shown as a number (DXV-07). The class labels were defined for heart failure without significant valve disease.
- **Numbers owned by a pack are referenced, not restated.** Diabetes types and gestational criteria come from the diabetes pack's signed REF-DM entries. BP grades and the crisis threshold come from the hypertension pack. The subtype file points to them, so a number never lives in two places.

## 4. Interaction (R6)

### 4.1 What each drop means

| Drag                              | Drop on                             | Meaning                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A co-existing or linked diagnosis | Another diagnosis card              | "This diagnosis relates to that one" (the dragged one is the condition; the target is the related condition) |
| A linked diagnosis                | The "Also this visit" header        | Unlink: it becomes co-existing                                                                               |
| A diagnosis                       | A background chip                   | Relate it to the known condition (DKA onto known type 1 diabetes)                                            |
| A background chip                 | Leading or "Also this visit"        | "Active this visit" (`episode_of`)                                                                           |
| A linked diagnosis                | Up or down within its parent's list | Reorder (sets `position`; the note follows the order)                                                        |

### 4.2 Choosing the relation on drop (D-REL-06)

1. **The pair is in a signed catalogue** (complication, cause or goes_with) **and is not direction-ambiguous.** Apply that relation at once, then show it inline on the card in words: "Linked as: secondary to malaria · Change · Undo".
2. **The catalogue lists the reverse direction** (B causes A, not A caused by B). Open the chooser with "Did you mean: _A_ secondary to _B_?" first.
3. **The pair is direction-ambiguous.** The builder marks these: AF with heart failure, AKI with acute heart failure, and stroke with very high BP, where an acute stroke raises BP and very high BP causes stroke. Always open the chooser (DXV-06).
4. **The pair is not in any catalogue.** Open a small centred chooser with no preselection and four choices: "is secondary to", "is a complication of", "was precipitated by", "is associated with (not a cause)". There is a fifth button, "Leave unrelated (co-existing)", and Cancel. An uncatalogued causal claim is the clinician's, explicitly.

The chooser is a centred dialog: nothing slides in from an edge, motion is 150 ms ease-out, and focus returns to the dropped card.

### 4.3 Undo, keyboard, touch and screen readers

- **Undo.** While the note is a draft, every relation change has an inline Undo on the card, with no timer and no toast; draft history also allows stepping back. After signing, a relation can only be retired or entered in error, with a reason.
- **Keyboard.**
  - Every card has a **"Relate to…"** menu item. It opens a centred dialog listing the possible targets (each with the catalogue's suggested relation first) and the relation choices.
  - "Move up" and "Move down" reorder within a parent. "Unlink" moves a linked card to co-existing.
  - Direct manipulation follows the WAI-ARIA drag-and-drop pattern: Space lifts a card, the arrow keys move between targets, Enter drops, Escape cancels.
- **Touch.** A long-press of about 500 ms lifts the card. Targets show their meaning in words while it hovers ("Drop to link under Malaria, severe"). The same "Relate to…" menu is one tap away for anyone who prefers it. Mis-drops are reversible with the inline Undo.
- **Screen readers.** A polite live region announces every outcome in words. For example: "Anaemia linked to Malaria, severe, as secondary to. Undo available." "Drop refused: Malaria, severe is a differential. Promote it first." "Link to Sepsis refused: it would make a loop."

### 4.4 What never drags, and what is refused

- **Never drags:**
  - anything in a signed note;
  - a row hidden from the viewer by a restricted class (it is not rendered);
  - a differential (use its menu: promote or mark Unlikely);
  - a refuted or entered-in-error item.
- **Refused, with a plain message:**
  - a self-link;
  - a link that would make a loop, or a fifth level of causes;
  - a link to or from a differential;
  - a link to a condition in a class the user cannot view.

## 5. Storage

### 5.1 Recommendation: a record-level table (D-REL-02)

Note-level `meta.with` cannot point at a problem-list condition (R5), cannot carry to the next visit, and is not audited as a clinical assertion. Relations drive counting and death-certificate proposals, so they must be append-only, versioned and protected by row-level security like `conditions`.

The DDL below assumes `conditions` has a stable logical id per condition that all its versions share. Where the column has a different name, point the foreign keys at it: relations must survive refinement. Replace the helper names (`app.current_hospital_id()`, `app.has_clinical_relationship()`, `app.can_view_classes()`, `app.audit_row()`) with the ones the `conditions` policies already use. The migration is **gated**: the owner applies it, and its number comes from the repository at build time.

```sql
-- NNNN_condition_relations.sql  (GATED: the owner applies it; never edit once shipped)

create type condition_relation_type  as enum ('due_to', 'associated_with', 'risk_factor_for', 'episode_of');
create type condition_relation_label as enum ('complication', 'secondary', 'precipitated_by');
create type condition_relation_state as enum ('active', 'retired', 'entered_in_error');

create table condition_relations (
  row_id               uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  relation_id          uuid not null,                       -- logical id shared by every version of this relation
  version              integer not null check (version >= 1),
  supersedes_row_id    uuid references condition_relations (row_id),
  hospital_id          uuid not null,
  patient_id           uuid not null,
  encounter_id         uuid not null,                       -- the visit at which it was asserted
  condition_id         uuid not null,                       -- logical id: the condition the relation describes (the effect, the factor, the this-visit episode)
  related_condition_id uuid not null,                       -- logical id: the cause, the outcome, the problem-list condition
  relation_type        condition_relation_type not null,
  label                condition_relation_label,            -- due_to only
  position             smallint not null default 1 check (position between 1 and 20),
  qualifiers           text[] not null default '{}',        -- coded non-diagnosis causes, e.g. {cause.insulin_omission}
  restricted_classes   text[] not null default '{}',        -- union of both ends' classes; set by trigger, never by the client
  state                condition_relation_state not null default 'active',
  state_reason         text,
  origin               text not null check (origin in ('clinician', 'system_end_removed', 'system_merge', 'backfill_confirmed')),
  catalogue_ref        text,                                -- e.g. 'MS-EMR-DX-CAUSES-001@1.0.0:dx.anaemia<-dx.malaria_severe' when offered from a catalogue
  asserted_by          uuid not null,
  asserted_at          timestamptz not null default now(),
  cosigned_by          uuid,
  cosigned_at          timestamptz,
  constraint cr_version_unique unique (relation_id, version),
  constraint cr_no_self        check (condition_id <> related_condition_id),
  constraint cr_label_rule     check ((relation_type = 'due_to') = (label is not null)),
  constraint cr_reason_rule    check (state = 'active' or state_reason is not null),
  constraint cr_cosign_pair    check ((cosigned_by is null) = (cosigned_at is null)),
  constraint cr_symmetric_once check (relation_type <> 'associated_with' or condition_id < related_condition_id)
);

create index cr_patient_state  on condition_relations (patient_id, state);
create index cr_condition      on condition_relations (condition_id);
create index cr_related        on condition_relations (related_condition_id);
create index cr_encounter      on condition_relations (encounter_id);
create index cr_relation_ver   on condition_relations (relation_id, version desc);

-- Current version of each relation
create view condition_relations_current as
  select distinct on (relation_id) *
  from condition_relations
  order by relation_id, version desc;

-- Append-only: no update, no delete, for anyone
create or replace function cr_append_only() returns trigger language plpgsql as $$
begin
  raise exception 'condition_relations is append-only; insert a new version instead';
end $$;
create trigger cr_no_update before update or delete on condition_relations
  for each row execute function cr_append_only();

-- Integrity on insert: same patient and hospital, ends not differential or refuted, no cycle, depth <= 4,
-- restricted classes computed from both ends.
create or replace function cr_check_insert() returns trigger language plpgsql as $$
declare
  a record; b record; depth int; below int;
begin
  if new.condition_id = new.related_condition_id then
    raise exception 'a diagnosis cannot be linked to itself';
  end if;
  select * into a from conditions_current where logical_id = new.condition_id;          -- adapt names
  select * into b from conditions_current where logical_id = new.related_condition_id;
  if a is null or b is null then raise exception 'both conditions must exist'; end if;
  if a.patient_id <> new.patient_id or b.patient_id <> new.patient_id
     or a.hospital_id <> new.hospital_id or b.hospital_id <> new.hospital_id then
    raise exception 'relation must join two conditions of the same patient and hospital';
  end if;
  if new.state = 'active' and new.relation_type in ('due_to', 'associated_with')
     and (a.verification in ('differential', 'refuted') or b.verification in ('differential', 'refuted')) then
    raise exception 'relations join working or final diagnoses only (D-REL-08)';
  end if;
  new.restricted_classes := (
    select coalesce(array_agg(distinct c), '{}')
    from unnest(array[a.restricted_class, b.restricted_class]) c
    where c is not null);
  if new.state = 'active' and new.relation_type = 'due_to' then
    with recursive up(id, d) as (
      select new.related_condition_id, 1
      union all
      select r.related_condition_id, up.d + 1
      from condition_relations_current r join up on r.condition_id = up.id
      where r.relation_type = 'due_to' and r.state = 'active' and up.d < 10)
    select max(d) into depth from up;
    if exists (
      with recursive up(id) as (
        select new.related_condition_id
        union
        select r.related_condition_id
        from condition_relations_current r join up on r.condition_id = up.id
        where r.relation_type = 'due_to' and r.state = 'active')
      select 1 from up where id = new.condition_id) then
      raise exception 'this link would make a loop';
    end if;
    -- effects already hanging below this condition count too (a link added in the middle lengthens their chains)
    with recursive down(id, d) as (
      select new.condition_id, 0
      union all
      select r.condition_id, down.d + 1
      from condition_relations_current r join down on r.related_condition_id = down.id
      where r.relation_type = 'due_to' and r.state = 'active' and down.d < 10)
    select max(d) into below from down;
    if depth + 1 + below > 4 then raise exception 'a cause chain is limited to 4 levels'; end if;
  end if;
  return new;
end $$;
create trigger cr_check before insert on condition_relations
  for each row execute function cr_check_insert();

-- Row-level security: hospital, clinical relationship, and every restricted class of both ends
alter table condition_relations enable row level security;
create policy cr_select on condition_relations for select using (
  hospital_id = app.current_hospital_id()
  and app.has_clinical_relationship(patient_id)
  and app.can_view_classes(restricted_classes));
create policy cr_insert on condition_relations for insert with check (
  hospital_id = app.current_hospital_id()
  and app.has_clinical_relationship(patient_id)
  and asserted_by = auth.uid()
  and origin = 'clinician');
-- No update or delete policy. System retirements (origin system_end_removed or system_merge) are inserted by a
-- security-definer function called from the conditions' entered-in-error and merge paths, and are audited.

create trigger cr_audit after insert on condition_relations
  for each row execute function app.audit_row();
```

Note on `cr_insert`: `restricted_classes` is set by the trigger, so the insert policy cannot check it from the client's row. The select policy is what hides restricted relations. In addition, the server action that creates a relation must check that the user can view both conditions before inserting.

### 5.2 Migrating `meta.with` and `flagged` (D-REL-17)

- **Unsigned drafts.** A one-off script turns each `meta[parent].with` entry into a relation:
  - `due_to` with label `complication` where the pair is a signed complication;
  - `associated_with` where it is a goes_with pair;
  - for anything else, the clinician is asked when the draft is next opened.

  `flagged` becomes a flag on the linked condition, not on the relation (it was a property of the diagnosis). The script's rows carry origin `backfill_confirmed` only after the clinician confirms them in the draft.

- **Signed notes.** Nothing is created. Their text already says "With: …". A row is created only if the encounter is reopened and the clinician confirms. A signed record must not gain clinical assertions afterwards.

### 5.3 What the problem list and later visits show

- The problem list nests confirmed chronic children under their parent ("Type 2 diabetes — diabetic retinopathy; diabetic kidney disease"). Acute relations (anaemia secondary to malaria) stay with their encounter.
- A later visit's board shows background chips with their relations, collapsed. The relations panel on a problem-list item shows its history: which visits had an `episode_of` it, and which complications were linked.

### 5.4 Edge cases

- **A relation to a condition from an earlier visit.** Allowed if that condition is still active, or was resolved within a recent window (proposed: 90 days, CSO to confirm). Example: anaemia today secondary to last week's malaria. The note writes "secondary to malaria (treated 3 October)".
- **A merge of duplicate patients or conditions.** Relations are re-pointed by a system version with origin `system_merge`. A relation that becomes a self-link or a loop after the merge is retired with a reason and shown for review.
- **A parent entered in error.** As in §1.2: the system retires the relation, and the child shows "Cause removed: review" (DXV-13).
- **A relation whose classes the user lost access to.** It disappears from that user's view. Nothing is altered.
- **Two clinicians editing the same draft.** Relations are versioned like conditions. The second save on a stale version is refused and the board refreshes.

## 6. Risks, and what not to build

| Risk                         | Clinical-safety reason                                                                                                                                                             | Design answer                                                                                                                                                                            |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The system "decides" a cause | Anchoring and premature closure: a suggested cause is accepted without thought, and the true cause (for example an upper GI bleed behind "anaemia secondary to malaria") is missed | Suggestions only; uncatalogued pairs need an explicit choice; "secondary to" never auto-applies to an uncatalogued pair; the anaemia work-up stays visible until refined                 |
| A wrong arrow travels        | Relations feed certificates and reports; a wrong chain misattributes deaths                                                                                                        | The certificate chain is proposed for the doctor to confirm; relations are versioned and audited; a removed cause is shown for review, not silently dropped                              |
| Restricted conditions leak   | Through note text ("secondary to HIV") or inference                                                                                                                                | Relations carry both ends' classes; row-level security returns nothing to a viewer without access; no placeholders; AIDS-defining illnesses stay visible to the treating team (D-REL-10) |
| Double counting              | Refinement stored as two conditions, or placeholders kept beside the diagnoses that explain them                                                                                   | Refinement is a version; placeholders are refined; risk factors and background are never counted                                                                                         |
| Severity churn               | Every new haemoglobin creating a new diagnosis version                                                                                                                             | Severity is a stage                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Over-linking and noise       | Every possible risk factor and association dragged in, burying the main diagnosis                                                                                                  | Risk factors are one derived line; linked children collapse after 3; the catalogue offers only pairs whose suggest_when is met                                                           |
| Mis-drop on touch            | A finger slips and creates a causal claim                                                                                                                                          | Inline wording of every link, Undo with no timer, long-press to lift, and the "Relate to…" menu as an alternative                                                                        |
| Blank read as negative       | A missing baseline creatinine read as "stage 1", a missing pregnancy status read as "not pregnant"                                                                                 | Stages are "insufficient" on any missing input                                                                                                                                           |

**Do not build:**

- automatic causal linking;
- free-text relation types (they cannot be counted or audited);
- relations from or to differentials;
- risk scores computed from relations;
- drag on signed notes;
- colour-only relation markers;
- a placeholder for hidden restricted causes;
- any alarm raised by a relation.

## 7. What is not settled here

- **ICD-11 codes and post-coordination.** Every ICD-11 code in the proposed terms, and every post-coordination pairing, is a candidate: check it against the WHO ICD-11 MMS API before use.
- **FHIR extension URLs** (`condition-dueTo`, `condition-related`, `condition-occurredFollowing`): check them against the R4 extension registry.
- **HMIS form behaviour.** Whether the OPD return separates new cases from revisits, and how inpatient secondary diagnoses are returned, should be confirmed with the HMIS team before D-REL-07 is built.
- **Uganda Clinical Guidelines 2023 was not opened.** Where a UCG section should be the source (anaemia, malaria, heart failure, stroke), the CSO names it, or confirms the cited reference.
- **Hypertension.** The grade and crisis threshold are the hypertension pack's to sign (D-HTN-01 and D-HTN-05). This file defers to it.
