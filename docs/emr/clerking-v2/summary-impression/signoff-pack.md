## 2. Must-not-miss reasons

| Diagnosis                                              | Why it must not be missed                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Accidental hypothermia                                 | Moderate and severe hypothermia cause arrhythmia and cardiac arrest; rewarming and careful handling must start at once.                                                      |
| Acute appendicitis                                     | Perforation and peritonitis follow delay, and in women a ruptured ectopic pregnancy presents the same way.                                                                   |
| Acute cholangitis                                      | Biliary sepsis that progresses to septic shock within hours without antibiotics and drainage.                                                                                |
| Acute coronary syndrome                                | Missed myocardial infarction or unstable angina carries high early mortality, and the benefit of reperfusion and antithrombotic treatment falls with every hour.             |
| Acute decompensated heart failure                      | Acute pulmonary oedema causes hypoxaemic respiratory failure and death within hours unless oxygenation, ventilation and the trigger are treated.                             |
| Acute limb ischaemia                                   | Muscle and nerve die within about six hours of complete arterial occlusion; revascularisation must not wait.                                                                 |
| Acute liver failure                                    | Cerebral oedema, bleeding, hypoglycaemia and sepsis kill within days; paracetamol and some other causes have specific time-dependent treatment.                              |
| Acute respiratory failure                              | Unrecognised respiratory failure progresses to arrest; oxygen, ventilatory support and treatment of the cause are needed now.                                                |
| Adrenal insufficiency                                  | Adrenal crisis (shock, hypoglycaemia, hyponatraemia) is fatal within hours yet cheap and quick to treat.                                                                     |
| Alcohol withdrawal                                     | Untreated withdrawal can progress to seizures and delirium tremens, which carry significant mortality, and Wernicke encephalopathy may coexist.                              |
| Anaphylaxis                                            | Airway obstruction, bronchospasm and circulatory collapse can kill within minutes; immediate adrenaline is the treatment.                                                    |
| Angioedema                                             | Tongue, floor-of-mouth and laryngeal swelling can obstruct the airway within minutes to hours.                                                                               |
| Aortic dissection                                      | Untreated type A dissection has a high hourly mortality, and treating it as ACS with antithrombotics or thrombolysis can be fatal.                                           |
| Bacterial meningitis                                   | Death or lasting disability rises with each hour of delayed antibiotics.                                                                                                     |
| Cardiac tamponade                                      | Tamponade causes obstructive shock and cardiac arrest; bedside echo and pericardiocentesis are life-saving.                                                                  |
| Cardiogenic shock                                      | Mortality is high and rises with every hour of hypoperfusion; treating it as hypovolaemia with large fluid volumes makes it worse.                                           |
| Cerebral malaria                                       | Coma with parasitaemia has high mortality; treatment and glucose correction cannot wait for other results.                                                                   |
| Cerebral toxoplasmosis                                 | The commonest focal brain lesion in advanced HIV; it responds to treatment and is fatal without it.                                                                          |
| Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis                       | Protean and easily missed; untreated it causes venous infarction, haemorrhage and death, and it needs specific venous imaging and anticoagulation.                           |
| Cholera                                                | Profuse watery diarrhoea can kill from dehydration within hours, and one case is a notifiable outbreak signal.                                                               |
| Complete heart block                                   | Complete heart block causes syncope, cardiac arrest and sudden death; pacing or chronotropic support is time-critical.                                                       |
| Cryptococcal meningitis                                | The leading cause of adult meningitis in Ugandan advanced HIV; raised pressure kills and is relieved by therapeutic LP.                                                      |
| Deep vein thrombosis                                   | Untreated proximal DVT embolises to the lungs; anticoagulation prevents fatal PE.                                                                                            |
| Diabetic ketoacidosis                                  | Fatal within hours without fluids, insulin and potassium management; the abdominal pain and vomiting are easily mistaken for a surgical abdomen or gastroenteritis.          |
| Dog bite, possible rabies exposure                     | Clinical rabies is almost always fatal; timely wound care and post-exposure prophylaxis prevent it.                                                                          |
| Drug-induced liver injury                              | Continuing the culprit (anti-TB drugs, nevirapine, herbal remedies) can progress to acute liver failure; stopping it is the key action.                                      |
| Eclampsia                                              | A leading cause of maternal death in Uganda; recurrent fits, stroke and fetal death follow within hours without treatment and delivery.                                      |
| Ectopic pregnancy                                      | Rupture causes haemorrhagic shock and is a leading cause of maternal death in Uganda; a pregnancy test and surgery save lives.                                               |
| Encephalitis                                           | HSV encephalitis is treatable and causes death or lasting brain damage when antiviral treatment is delayed.                                                                  |
| Febrile neutropenia                                    | Can progress to septic shock within hours; empirical antibiotics should not wait for the blood count.                                                                        |
| Gastrointestinal bleeding, source not yet known        | Upper or lower GI bleeding can exsanguinate within hours; source and haemodynamics decide the next hour.                                                                     |
| Giant cell arteritis                                   | Can cause sudden permanent blindness in one or both eyes; treatment on suspicion prevents it.                                                                                |
| Guillain-Barré syndrome                                | Respiratory and bulbar failure and autonomic instability can develop within hours to days; early immunotherapy and monitoring change outcome.                                |
| Heat stroke                                            | Brain, muscle, kidney and clotting injury accumulate with every minute of hyperthermia; cooling must begin at once.                                                          |
| Hepatic encephalopathy                                 | Marks decompensation and usually has a treatable precipitant (GI bleed, SBP or other infection, hypokalaemia); grades III-IV threaten the airway.                            |
| Hyperkalaemia                                          | Causes bradyarrhythmia and cardiac arrest without warning symptoms.                                                                                                          |
| Hyperosmolar hyperglycaemic state                      | Profound dehydration with high mortality, especially in older adults; thrombosis and overly rapid osmolality shifts add harm.                                                |
| Hypertensive crisis                                    | Hypertensive emergency with acute organ damage (encephalopathy, stroke, pulmonary oedema, dissection, eclampsia, AKI) kills within hours without controlled BP reduction.    |
| Hypoglycaemia in a person with diabetes                | Causes seizures, brain injury and death within minutes to hours and is reversed immediately by glucose.                                                                      |
| Hypoglycaemia, no known diabetes                       | Causes seizures, brain injury and death within hours, and usually signals a serious cause (severe malaria, sepsis, liver failure, adrenal insufficiency).                    |
| Hypovolaemic shock                                     | Shock from haemorrhage or fluid loss is fatal within hours unless volume is restored and the source controlled.                                                              |
| Infective endocarditis                                 | Curable if found but destroys valves and embolises; rheumatic heart disease makes it common in young Ugandan adults.                                                         |
| Intestinal obstruction                                 | Strangulation, ischaemia and perforation develop within hours; needs early surgical review.                                                                                  |
| Intracerebral haemorrhage                              | Haematoma expansion in the first hours worsens outcome; blood pressure control and reversal of any bleeding tendency are urgent, and antithrombotics must be avoided.        |
| Intracranial space-occupying lesion                    | Raised intracranial pressure can cause herniation, and the commonest lesions in advanced HIV (toxoplasmosis, tuberculoma) are treatable.                                     |
| Ischaemic stroke                                       | Reperfusion and secondary prevention are time-dependent, and an embolic source (AF, rheumatic valve disease, endocarditis) needs its own treatment.                          |
| Leukaemia                                              | Acute leukaemia kills through bleeding and neutropenic sepsis within days, and a full blood count separates it; chronic forms are not time-critical.                         |
| Malaria, severe                                        | High mortality that falls with prompt parenteral treatment; any single WHO severity criterion makes it an emergency.                                                         |
| Meningitis, cause not yet known                        | Bacterial, cryptococcal and TB meningitis kill quickly and each needs different urgent treatment; delay to antimicrobials while awaiting LP or CT raises mortality.          |
| Methanol poisoning                                     | Untreated methanol causes severe metabolic acidosis, permanent blindness and death; antidote and dialysis are time-dependent.                                                |
| Myxoedema coma                                         | Hypothermia, bradycardia, hypoventilation and hyponatraemia progress to death without thyroid hormone and supportive care; it is often mistaken for sepsis or stroke.        |
| Necrotising soft-tissue infection                      | Mortality rises with every hour before surgical debridement, and early signs are subtle.                                                                                     |
| Oncological emergency, suspected                       | Cord compression, hypercalcaemia, SVC obstruction and tumour lysis cause paraplegia, arrhythmia, coma or renal failure within hours to days.                                 |
| Opioid or sedative toxicity                            | Respiratory depression leads to hypoxic arrest; it is reversible with airway support and, for opioids, an antagonist.                                                        |
| Organophosphate or carbamate poisoning                 | Bronchorrhoea, bronchospasm and respiratory muscle paralysis cause death within hours; antidote and airway care are urgent.                                                  |
| Paracetamol overdose                                   | Liver failure is preventable only if the antidote starts early, best within 8 hours of ingestion; patients look well until it is too late.                                   |
| Perforated viscus                                      | Generalised peritonitis and septic shock within hours; needs resuscitation, antibiotics and surgery.                                                                         |
| Pneumocystis pneumonia                                 | Hypoxic respiratory failure with high mortality in untreated advanced HIV; treatable, and adjunctive therapy depends on recognising hypoxia early.                           |
| Pneumothorax                                           | Tension pneumothorax causes obstructive shock and arrest within minutes; it is a clinical diagnosis needing immediate decompression.                                         |
| Poisoning by a non-medicinal substance                 | Pesticide, paraffin, caustic and toxic-alcohol poisoning cause airway, respiratory and metabolic collapse within hours.                                                      |
| Poisoning or overdose by a medicine                    | Sedative, cardiotoxic and hypoglycaemic overdoses kill within hours, and paracetamol injury is silent until too late to prevent.                                             |
| Poisoning, substance not yet known                     | Many poisons in Uganda (pesticides, sedatives, toxic alcohols) cause respiratory failure, fits or arrhythmia within hours; supportive care and antidotes are time-dependent. |
| Pre-eclampsia                                          | A leading cause of maternal death in Uganda; progression to severe disease and eclampsia is preventable when recognised.                                                     |
| Pulmonary embolism                                     | Untreated PE has high early mortality and massive PE kills within hours; anticoagulation or thrombolysis is effective.                                                       |
| Ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm                     | A ruptured aortic aneurysm is fatal within hours without surgery and is often misdiagnosed as renal colic or back strain.                                                    |
| Sepsis                                                 | Mortality rises with every hour of delayed antimicrobials and fluids.                                                                                                        |
| Septic arthritis                                       | Destroys the joint within days and can cause sepsis; aspirate before antibiotics.                                                                                            |
| Septic shock                                           | Refractory hypotension with hypoperfusion; mortality is high and time-dependent.                                                                                             |
| Severe pre-eclampsia                                   | Imminent risk of eclampsia, stroke, HELLP, pulmonary oedema and abruption; seizure prevention and delivery planning cannot wait.                                             |
| Sickle cell crisis                                     | Acute chest syndrome, splenic sequestration, aplastic crisis and stroke kill within hours to days and need specific treatment beyond analgesia.                              |
| Snakebite envenoming                                   | Coagulopathy, neurotoxic paralysis and AKI can develop over hours after a quiet start; antivenom works best early.                                                           |
| Spinal cord or cauda equina compression                | Paralysis and sphincter loss become permanent if decompression or treatment is delayed; function at treatment predicts outcome.                                              |
| Spontaneous bacterial peritonitis                      | Often silent, and untreated SBP precipitates kidney failure, encephalopathy and death within days.                                                                           |
| Status epilepticus                                     | Continuous or repeated seizures cause neuronal injury, aspiration and death, and become harder to stop the longer they last.                                                 |
| Stevens-Johnson syndrome or toxic epidermal necrolysis | Mortality is high and rises with delay in withdrawing the culprit drug; eye and mucosal involvement scars permanently.                                                       |
| Stroke                                                 | Time-dependent treatment and complications (aspiration, raised pressure, glucose and BP management) and mimics such as hypoglycaemia that need different treatment.          |
| Subarachnoid haemorrhage                               | Early rebleeding is often fatal; a warning bleed with a thunderclap headache is the chance to find and secure the aneurysm.                                                  |
| Subdural haematoma                                     | A surgically treatable cause of drowsiness and focal signs that can deteriorate to coning within hours.                                                                      |
| Suicidal thoughts or self-harm                         | Missed intent can end in death within hours; asking directly, supervision and removal of means prevent it.                                                                   |
| Tetanus                                                | Spasms obstruct the airway and autonomic instability kills; the diagnosis is clinical and no test excludes it.                                                               |
| Thyroid storm                                          | Hyperthermia, tachyarrhythmia and heart failure are fatal within hours to days without specific treatment.                                                                   |
| Transient ischaemic attack                             | The risk of a disabling stroke is highest in the first days; urgent assessment and secondary prevention prevent it.                                                          |
| Tuberculous meningitis                                 | Outcome depends on the stage at which treatment starts; a negative CSF Xpert does not exclude it.                                                                            |
| Tuberculous pericarditis                               | Effusive disease can tamponade and constriction is disabling; the commonest cause of pericardial effusion in Uganda.                                                         |
| Upper airway obstruction                               | Complete airway obstruction kills within minutes; stridor needs immediate senior airway assessment, not investigations.                                                      |
| Variceal bleeding                                      | Exsanguination within hours; needs resuscitation and urgent endoscopy.                                                                                                       |
| Ventricular tachycardia or other malignant arrhythmia  | VT degenerates into VF and cardiac arrest; a broad-complex tachycardia is VT until proven otherwise.                                                                         |
| Viral haemorrhagic fever, suspected                    | High mortality and onward transmission to staff and family; isolate and notify on suspicion, before any other workup.                                                        |
| Wernicke encephalopathy                                | Untreated it progresses to permanent amnesia (Korsakoff) or death; in at-risk patients give thiamine alongside glucose, never delaying glucose for hypoglycaemia.            |
