## Notes

1. **Hypoglycaemia.** UCG 1.1.6 and ADA level 2 agree at 3.0 mmol/L, so `FLAG-EX-HYPO-L2` ("treat now") has both a Uganda and an ADA basis. Level 1 (<3.9) is ADA-only, and ADA writes it for people with diabetes. ADA rec 6.15: "Glucose is the preferred treatment for the conscious individual with glucose <70 mg/dL (<3.9 mmol/L)". UCG does not call 3.0–3.9 hypoglycaemia. The CSO should decide whether `FLAG-EX-HYPO-L1` keeps "treat now" for every adult, or only for people with diabetes (or on insulin or a sulfonylurea). Two further points:
   - UCG's own "(55 mg/dl)" is a rounding slip; 3.0 mmol/L is 54 mg/dL.
   - UCG's severe-malaria hypoglycaemia threshold (<2.2 mmol/L, p. 166) is disease-specific. It is not a general threshold.
2. **Euglycaemic DKA.** ADA 2026 §16: about 10% of DKA presents with glucose <11.1 mmol/L, so "DKA diagnosis requires either the presence of hyperglycemia or prior history of diabetes". `FLAG-EX-KETONES-SMELL` and the Kussmaul flag both require glucose ≥11.1, so they miss euglycaemic DKA (SGLT2 inhibitors, pregnancy, low intake). The CSO should consider an "or known diabetes" branch.
3. **ROSIER.** On the form, all five +1 items sit under "Is there a NEW ACUTE onset (or on awakening from sleep)?". The catalogue scores current positive findings (CN VII, pronator drift, asymmetric power, speech, fields) with no "new" qualifier, so an old stroke deficit would score. The CSO should consider extending the existing clinician-confirmation note (today only for syncope and seizure) to cover the signs. The form also says ROSIER "is not suitable for patients with suspected TIA with no neurological signs when seen".
4. **Ipswich touch test.** The ≥2 of 6 threshold counts both feet together. `iwgdf_risk` derives LOPS "per foot" using `REF-EX-ITT-LOPS`, but a single total cannot place LOPS on one foot (one insensate toe on each foot scores 2). Either apply the ITT result to both feet, or record the toes per foot.
5. **Fever threshold for VHF.** Neither WHO 2014 nor Uganda IDSR 2021 gives a number ("fever", "high fever"). For comparison:
   - UCG 2023 defines malarial fever as "above 37.5°C (taken from the axilla)" (p. 165).
   - The UVRI/MoH VHF surveillance definition uses ">38°C".

   The rule also fires on reported fever (`sym.fever = yes`), so the threshold matters only when fever is not reported. Options for the CSO: keep 38.0 inclusive as an operational value (slightly more sensitive than ">38"), or align to ">38". IDSR's outbreak definition also counts "any person with inexplicable bleeding" as suspected even without fever. The CSO may want an outbreak-mode variant of `FLAG-EX-FEVER-BLEEDING`.

6. **Access notes.**
   - `library.health.go.ug`: the TLS chain fails verification. The UCG PDF was downloaded with verification off; it is a 1,161-page PDF of 13.5 MB.
   - `diabetesjournals.org`, `thelancet.com` and `ahajournals.org` return 403 (Cloudflare). PMC and PubMed copies were used instead.
   - The `nice.org.uk` WebFetch returned 403, but a plain HTTP fetch worked.
   - TRS 854 is image-only, so it was read with macOS Vision OCR.
